1. **A paravector has no intrinsic spin assignment without a transformation law.**

This is stronger than identifying spin solely from a sign change after a full rotation: other half-integer representations also change sign.

Setting `q'=0` removes one coupling; it does not make the imaginary product or the magnetic field vanish. Magnetic sources extend Maxwell’s equation while spacetime stays real. A single smooth global vector potential then becomes inadequate, as in the standard [monopole and gauge-patch treatment](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gaugetheory/1em.pdf).

If `P=mU` stays real, its imaginary equation imposes an extra field constraint that ordinary electromagnetic motion does not satisfy. If `P` is an independent complex variable, the equation merely accumulates two impulses and does not specify a trajectory. Closing it with complex `U=P/m` gives the exact constant-field solution `P(s)=exp((q+i q')sF/m)P(0)` and preserves its complex determinant.

    But that closed proposal fails a decisive ordinary limit: for `q'=0`, a particle initially at rest in a constant magnetic field acquires `P(s)=m cos(qBs/m)+i m sigma3 sin(qBs/m)`. Its determinant stays `m²`, while its real energy changes and its imaginary momentum oscillates. Integrating `dZ/ds=P/m` makes imaginary spatial displacement explicit. Therefore zero magnetic charge does not restore real spacetime in this proposal, and determinant conservation is insufficient to establish physical causality.

    The established real-force equation has a useful exact solution that retains the full complex field: `P(s)=T(s)P(0)T(s)^H`, with `T(s)=exp((q-i q')sF/(2m))`.
