\subsubsection{Intertwining and the General Similarity Transform}
Compatibility between two descriptions of the same algebraic action is
expressed by the starting relation \eqref{eq:transform-intertwining}.
An arbitrary algebra input
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'\) in the primed description is
represented by \(\pv{T}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'\) in the
unprimed description. Acting first with
\(\pv{Z}'\) and then changing description must give the same result as
changing description first and then acting with \(\pv{Z}\):
\begin{equation*}
\pv{T}(\pv{Z}'\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}')
=\pv{Z}(\pv{T}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}').
\end{equation*}
This equality holds for every
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'\).
Setting the arbitrary right factor to \(1\) gives
\(\pv{T}\pv{Z}'=\pv{Z}\pv{T}\). This compatibility equation is conventionally
called an intertwining relation. Because \(\pv{T}\) is invertible, its
unique solution is
\begin{equation*}
\pv{Z}'=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{Z}\pv{T}.
\end{equation*}
Thus Eq.\ \eqref{eq:general-transform} adds no law of motion. It is the
change-of-description rule forced by the displayed compatibility
requirement, and its inverse-pair form is called a similarity transform.
When the direction of the identification is reversed, \(\pv{T}\) is
replaced everywhere by
\(\pv{T}^{-1}\), and the equivalent convention
\(\pv{Z}'=\pv{T}\pv{Z}\pv{T}^{-1}\) is obtained. Under the inverse-first
convention, the displayed positive sign in Rodrigues's formula is produced
by the positive exponent in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:rotor-element}
\cite{doran2003}.

The fully general complex paravectors
\(\pv{Z},\pv{T}\in\CPspace\) are written as
\begin{align*}
\pv{Z}&=S+\vct{V}\cdot\sigv,
&S&\in\C,
&\vct{V}&\in\C^3,\\
\pv{T}&=f+\vct{g}\cdot\sigv,
&f&\in\C,
&\vct{g}&\in\C^3,
\end{align*}
where the ordinary dot and cross products are expanded linearly in each
input, with no complex conjugation of either input. This is the meaning of
their extension to complex vectors. The determinant
\(\detf{\pv{T}}=f^2-\vct{g}^2\) is a complex scalar and therefore commutes
with every algebra element. When
\(\detf{\pv{T}}\neq0\), the inverse is given by the general formula
\eqref{eq:paravector-inverse}:
\begin{equation*}
\pv{T}^{-1}=\frac{\cherm{\pv{T}}}{\detf{\pv{T}}}
=\frac{f-\vct{g}\cdot\sigv}{f^2-\vct{g}^2}.
\end{equation*}
Because the scalar part \(S\) is central,
\begin{align*}
\pv{Z}'
&=S+\frac{(f-\vct{g}\cdot\sigv)
 (\vct{V}\cdot\sigv)(f+\vct{g}\cdot\sigv)}{\detf{\pv{T}}}.
\end{align*}
The first multiplication is
\begin{equation*}
(f-\vct{g}\cdot\sigv)(\vct{V}\cdot\sigv)
=-\vct{g}\cdot\vct{V}
+\bigl(f\vct{V}-i\,\vct{g}\times\vct{V}\bigr)\cdot\sigv.
\end{equation*}
After multiplication by the final factor, the scalar terms cancel and the
remaining vector terms are
\begin{align}
\pv{Z}'&=S+\vct{V}'\cdot\sigv,\notag\\
\vct{V}'
&=\frac{(f^2+\vct{g}^2)\vct{V}
-2(\vct{g}\cdot\vct{V})\vct{g}
-2i\,f(\vct{g}\times\vct{V})}{f^2-\vct{g}^2}.
\label{eq:similarity-components}
\end{align}
This is the similarity law for every complex paravector \(\pv{Z}\) and every
invertible complex paravector \(\pv{T}\). The complex scalar part is fixed.
Neither Eq.\ \eqref{eq:general-transform} nor
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:similarity-components} is changed when \(\pv{T}\) is multiplied
by a nonzero central complex scalar.

Products, inverses, and functions are governed by the same intertwining. For a
second algebra element \(\pv{W}\),
\begin{align}
(\pv{Z}\pv{W})'&=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{Z}\pv{W}\pv{T}
=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{Z}\pv{T}\,\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{W}\pv{T}=\pv{Z}'\pv{W}',\notag\\
(\pv{Z}')^{-1}&=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{Z}^{-1}\pv{T}
\qquad\text{when }\pv{Z}^{-1}\text{ exists},\notag\\
(\pv{Z}')^n&=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{Z}^n\pv{T},\notag\\
h(\pv{Z}')&=\pv{T}^{-1}h(\pv{Z})\pv{T}.
\label{eq:similarity-covariance}
\end{align}
The last line is valid when \(h\) is given by a convergent power series, and
therefore in particular
\begin{equation*}
\exp(\pv{Z}')=\pv{T}^{-1}\exp(\pv{Z})\pv{T}.
\end{equation*}
The same relation is obeyed by logarithms, roots, and ratios of polynomials
wherever the same choice among their possible values is made and every
required inverse exists.

Applying the adjugate before the similarity gives the same result as
applying it afterward. Since
\(\cherm{\pv{T}}=\detf{\pv{T}}\pv{T}^{-1}\) and
\(\cherm{(\pv{T}^{-1})}=\pv{T}/\detf{\pv{T}}\),
\begin{align*}
\cherm{\pv{Z}'}
&=\cherm{\pv{T}}\,\cherm{\pv{Z}}\,\cherm{(\pv{T}^{-1})}
=\pv{T}^{-1}\cherm{\pv{Z}}\pv{T},\\
\pv{Z}'\cherm{\pv{Z}'}
&=\pv{T}^{-1}(\pv{Z}\cherm{\pv{Z}})\pv{T}=\pv{Z}\cherm{\pv{Z}}.
\end{align*}
Thus the determinant form \(S^2-\vct{V}^2\), the two scalar values defined
in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:complex-paravector-eigenvalues}, and the inverse formula
are preserved by similarity for every invertible \(\pv{T}\). The positive
coefficient norm is a different construction. It is not fixed by the
similarity equations and need not be preserved.

Algebra elements whose action is represented by multiplication are transported
by the similarity law; this law is not automatically the Lorentz law for every
physical use of the algebra. A real event paravector is characterized by
\(\pv{X}^{\Hop}=\pv{X}\). Pairing the chosen left factor
\(\pv{T}^{-1}\) with its Hermitian image on the right preserves that defining
property for every real event and reduces to the identity map when
\(\pv{T}=1\). Direct application of \(\Hop\) gives
\[
\left(
\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{X}(\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}
\right)^{\Hop}
=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{X}(\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}.
\]
The same law is exposed by any product
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}^{\Hop}\), which is self-Hermitian. If
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'
=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}\), then
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'^{\Hop}
&=\pv{T}^{-1}\notag\\
&\quad{}
\bigl(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}^{\Hop}\bigr)
(\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}.
\end{align*}
For an element that satisfies the determinant-one condition
\(\pv{T}\cherm{\pv{T}}=1\), as required for the rotations and boosts
considered here, it is
\begin{align}
\pv{X}'
&=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{X}(\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}\notag\\
&=\pv{T}^{*\Hop}\pv{X}\pv{T}^*.
\label{eq:general-event-congruence}
\end{align}
The right factor is the Hermitian image of the left factor. A two-sided
transformation with this relation between its factors is called a Hermitian
congruence \cite{baylis1999,doran2003}. It is not a similarity, whose two
factors are an inverse pair. The second factor occurs because an event can
be built from products
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}^{\Hop}\). The complex
scalar part of every paravector is
preserved by a similarity according to Eq.\ \eqref{eq:similarity-components};
the time coordinate therefore cannot be mixed with a spatial coordinate by
that law.
Distinct roles are assigned to star and \(\Hop\). Their combination
\({}^{*\Hop}\) is the adjugate, which equals \(\pv{T}^{-1}\) only after the
determinant-one normalization
\(\pv{T}\cherm{\pv{T}}=1\) has been imposed. Appendix
\ref{sec:matrix-appendix} gives the optional translation of these
operations.
The set \(\RPspace\) is preserved because
\((\pv{X}')^{\Hop}=\pv{X}'\), and the event determinant is preserved because
\(\pv{T}^{-1}\) has unit determinant. When \(\pv{X}\) is invertible, the
transformed inverse is obtained without a new definition:
\begin{equation}
(\pv{X}')^{-1}=\pv{T}^{\Hop}\pv{X}^{-1}\pv{T},
\label{eq:event-inverse-transform}
\end{equation}
because the identity is obtained by direct multiplication with
\(\pv{X}'=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{X}(\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}\). The same inverse
pattern is used later when the derivative paravector is transformed. An
equivalent event compatibility equation is obtained by
multiplication of Eq.\ \eqref{eq:general-event-congruence} on the left by
\(\pv{T}\) and on the right by \(\pv{T}^{\Hop}\):
\begin{align}
\pv{T}\pv{X}'\pv{T}^{\Hop}&=\pv{X},\notag\\
\pv{T}\pv{X}'&=\pv{X}\pv{T}^*.
\label{eq:event-intertwining}
\end{align}
The determinant-one identity
\((\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}=\pv{T}^*\) is used in the second line.
The form \(\pv{T}\pv{X}'=\pv{X}\pv{T}\) is obtained for a circular rotor because
\(\pv{R}^*=\pv{R}\), but not for a boost. The similarity and event
compatibility equations are therefore identical for circular rotors but
remain distinct for boosts.

In contrast, the electromagnetic field paravector is defined later by
\(\pv{F}=(\vct{E}+i\vct{B})\cdot\sigv\) in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:F-Phi}, where
\(\vct{E}\) and \(\vct{B}\) are the real electric and magnetic field
vectors. Its action is represented by left multiplication on an algebra
element. The following similarity is then required by the original
compatibility argument:
\begin{equation}
\pv{F}'=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{F}\pv{T}.
\label{eq:general-field-similarity}
\end{equation}
For a circular rotor, \(\pv{R}^*=\pv{R}\) and
\(\pv{R}^{-1}=\pv{R}^{\Hop}\), so Eq.\ \eqref{eq:general-event-congruence}
rotates the represented spatial vector while the coordinate axes are held
fixed. This description is called active, and the event law is reduced to
\begin{equation}
\pv{X}'=\herm{\pv{R}}\pv{X}\pv{R}.
\label{eq:rotor}
\end{equation}
For the hyperbolic element in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:boost-element},
\(\pv{L}^{\Hop}=\pv{L}\) and
\(\pv{L}^{-1}=\pv{L}^*=\pv{L}^{*\Hop}\). In this case the coordinates
assigned to the same event are changed to those of the boosted frame. This
description is called passive
\cite{lorentz1904,einstein1905,minkowski1908}, and the same general event law
is reduced to
\begin{align}
\pv{X}'
&=\pv{L}^{-1}\pv{X}(\pv{L}^{-1})^{\Hop}\notag\\
&=\conj{\pv{L}}\pv{X}\conj{\pv{L}}.
\label{eq:boost}
\end{align}
The equal factors in the final line are obtained from
\((\pv{L}^{-1})^{\Hop}=\pv{L}^{-1}\). They are not an inverse pair.
For comparison, the following results are obtained from the two possible
similarity orderings:
\begin{align}
\pv{L}^{-1}\pv{X}\pv{L}
&=t+\Bigl(
\eqfunc{par}(\vct{r})
+\cosh(\theta)\eqfunc{prp}(\vct{r})\notag\\
&\qquad
-i\sinh(\theta)\vct{u}\times\vct{r}
\Bigr)\!\cdot\sigv,\notag\\
\pv{L}\pv{X}\pv{L}^{-1}
&=t+\Bigl(
\eqfunc{par}(\vct{r})
+\cosh(\theta)\eqfunc{prp}(\vct{r})\notag\\
&\qquad
+i\sinh(\theta)\vct{u}\times\vct{r}
\Bigr)\!\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:event-boost-similarity-test}
\end{align}
Under both similarities, \(t\) and \(\eqfunc{par}(\vct{r})\) are left
unchanged and a generic real perpendicular component is made complex. A
Lorentz boost of the real event \(\pv{X}\) is therefore not obtained. In the
simple case \(\vct{r}=a\vct{u}\), where the position vector is parallel to
the boost axis,
\(\com{\pv{X}}{\pv{L}}=0\) is obtained, so the entire event is left unchanged
by both similarities instead of \(t\) and \(a\) being mixed.
By contrast,
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:general-field-similarity} is reduced to
\(\pv{F}'=\pv{L}^{-1}\pv{F}\pv{L}
=\pv{L}^*\pv{F}\pv{L}\).
The unequal right factors are therefore consequences of the event and field
having different algebraic actions, not of an inconsistent choice of
inverse. Under the definition of \(\pv{L}(\theta,\vct{u})\), the two-sided
product \(\pv{L}^{\Hop}\pv{X}\pv{L}
=\pv{L}\pv{X}\pv{L}\) is the inverse-direction event boost, obtained by
replacing \(\theta\) with \(-\theta\). It is physically legitimate, but it
is not the passive \(+\theta\) convention used in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:boost}.

The hyperbolic reduction of the general complex formula is obtained
directly with
\[
f=\cosh(\theta/2),
\qquad
\vct{g}=\sinh(\theta/2)\vct{u},
\qquad
\vct{u}^2=1.
\]
Substitution into Eq.\ \eqref{eq:similarity-components} gives
\begin{equation}
\vct{V}'
=(\vct{u}\cdot\vct{V})\vct{u}
+\cosh(\theta)\bigl(\vct{V}-(\vct{u}\cdot\vct{V})\vct{u}\bigr)
-i\sinh(\theta)\,\vct{u}\times\vct{V}.
\label{eq:hyperbolic-similarity}
\end{equation}
For \(\vct{V}=\vct{E}+i\vct{B}\), its real and imaginary parts are
exactly the standard electric--magnetic boost formulas
\cite{jackson1998}. For a real \(\vct{V}\),
however, the cross term is generally imaginary; hyperbolic similarity is
therefore a field or basis transformation rather than an event boost.

A transform element \(\pv{T}(\lambda)\) is taken to depend on one real
parameter \(\lambda\). All displayed derivatives are ordinary derivatives
with respect to that parameter. The quantity that measures the
parameter-rate of change of \(\pv{T}\), expressed on its inverse-carried
side, is defined by
\begin{equation}
\pv{\Omega}:=\pv{T}^{-1}\frac{\dd\pv{T}}{\dd\lambda}.
\label{eq:similarity-generator}
\end{equation}
It is called the similarity generator because it determines the
first-order change of every similarity-transformed element.
For a small parameter change, the local factor is
\begin{align*}
\pv{T}(\lambda)^{-1}\pv{T}(\lambda+\dd\lambda)
&=1+\pv{\Omega}\,\dd\lambda,\\[-0.2em]
&\quad\text{with terms containing }(\dd\lambda)^2\text{ discarded}.
\end{align*}
This first-order form is conventionally called infinitesimal. The
similarity and event actions of this local factor are
\begin{align*}
\pv{Z}(\lambda+\dd\lambda)
&=\pv{Z}(\lambda)
+\com{\pv{Z}(\lambda)}{\pv{\Omega}}\,\dd\lambda,\\
\pv{X}(\lambda+\dd\lambda)
&=\pv{X}(\lambda)-\pv{\Omega}\pv{X}(\lambda)\,\dd\lambda
-\pv{X}(\lambda)\pv{\Omega}^{\Hop}\,\dd\lambda.
\end{align*}
The two Hermitian cases reduce the added event term to
\begin{align*}
\pv{\Omega}^{\Hop}=-\pv{\Omega}
&:\quad \com{\pv{X}(\lambda)}{\pv{\Omega}}\,\dd\lambda,\\
\pv{\Omega}^{\Hop}=\pv{\Omega}
&:\quad -\acom{\pv{\Omega}}{\pv{X}(\lambda)}\,\dd\lambda.
\end{align*}
The first term generates a rotation. The second mixes the scalar time
component with the vector component along the boost direction.
Differentiation of the inverse and of the transported paravector then gives
\begin{align*}
\frac{\dd(\pv{T}^{-1})}{\dd\lambda}
&=-\pv{T}^{-1}\frac{\dd\pv{T}}{\dd\lambda}\pv{T}^{-1},\\
\frac{\dd\pv{Z}'}{\dd\lambda} &=
\pv{T}^{-1}\frac{\dd\pv{Z}}{\dd\lambda}\pv{T}
+\com{\pv{Z}'}{\pv{\Omega}},\notag \\
\frac{\dd^2\pv{Z}'}{\dd\lambda^2} &=
\pv{T}^{-1}\frac{\dd^2\pv{Z}}{\dd\lambda^2}\pv{T}\notag \\
&\quad +2\com{\dfrac{\dd\pv{Z}'}{\dd\lambda}}{\pv{\Omega}},\notag \\
&\quad +\com{\pv{Z}'}{\dfrac{\dd\pv{\Omega}}{\dd\lambda}},\notag \\
&\quad +\com{\pv{\Omega}}{\com{\pv{Z}'}{\pv{\Omega}}}.
\end{align*}
They are not space-time derivatives.

\subsubsection{Transformed Basis}
The parameter-dependent element is written as
\begin{align*}
\pv{T}(\lambda)&=f(\lambda)+\vct{g}(\lambda)\cdot\sigv,
\qquad f\in\C,\quad \vct{g}\in\C^3,\\
\pv{T}^{-1}&=\frac{f-\vct{g}\cdot\sigv}{f^2-\vct{g}^2},
\qquad f^2-\vct{g}^2\neq0.
\end{align*}
The transformed basis element is defined by
\begin{equation}
\pv{\sigma}_n':=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{\sigma}_n\pv{T}.
\label{eq:transformed-basis-definition}
\end{equation}
Its inverse relation is
\[
\pv{\sigma}_n=\pv{T}\pv{\sigma}_n'\pv{T}^{-1}.
\]
Both real hyperbolic elements and imaginary-vector rotors are included by
allowing complex \(\vct{g}\). The following relations are obtained
immediately by similarity:
\begin{align*}
(\pv{\sigma}_n')^2&=1,\\
\pv{\sigma}_n'\pv{\sigma}_m'
&=\delta_{nm}+\sum_{k=1}^3\epsilon_{nmk}\,i\pv{\sigma}_k',\\
\pv{\sigma}_1'\pv{\sigma}_2'\pv{\sigma}_3'&=i.
\end{align*}
Thus the same sigma multiplication table is satisfied by the transformed
generators for every invertible \(\pv{T}\).

The separate actions of star and \(\Hop\) on the transformed
generators are represented by
\begin{align*}
\conj{\pv{\sigma}_n'}
&=\conj{(\pv{T}^{-1})}\conj{\pv{\sigma}_n}\conj{\pv{T}}
=-(\conj{\pv{T}})^{-1}\pv{\sigma}_n\conj{\pv{T}},\\
\herm{\pv{\sigma}_n'}
&=\herm{\pv{T}}\herm{\pv{\sigma}_n}\herm{(\pv{T}^{-1})}
=\herm{\pv{T}}\pv{\sigma}_n(\herm{\pv{T}})^{-1}.
\end{align*}
The scalar part is also preserved by similarity, so
\(\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\sigma}_n')=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\sigma}_n)=0\).
The individual star and \(\Hop\) actions are recorded separately by these
formulas and are combined by the adjugate relation.

For every invertible \(\pv{T}\), applying the adjugate before the similarity
gives the same result as applying it afterward. This follows from
\(\cherm{\pv{T}}=(f^2-\vct{g}^2)\pv{T}^{-1}\) and
\(\cherm{(\pv{T}^{-1})}=\pv{T}/(f^2-\vct{g}^2)\):
\begin{align*}
(\pv{\sigma}_n')^{*\Hop}
&=(f^2-\vct{g}^2)\pv{T}^{-1}(-\pv{\sigma}_n)
  \frac{\pv{T}}{f^2-\vct{g}^2}\\
&=-\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{\sigma}_n\pv{T}=-\pv{\sigma}_n'.
\end{align*}
Consequently a paravector written in the transformed basis has the form
\[
\pv{Z}
=S+\vct{V}\cdot\sigv'.
\]
Here \(S\) and \(\vct{V}\) denote the coefficients in the displayed primed
basis; no fixed-basis vector extraction is being applied. The same
determinant expression is then assigned:
\[
\pv{Z}\pv{Z}^{*\Hop}
=S^2-\vct{V}^2.
\]

For differentiation with respect to \(\lambda\), the generator from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:similarity-generator} gives
\begin{align*}
\frac{\dd\pv{\sigma}_n'}{\dd\lambda}
&=\com{\pv{\sigma}_n'}{\pv{\Omega}},&
\frac{\dd\sigv'}{\dd\lambda}
&=\com{\sigv'}{\pv{\Omega}}.
\end{align*}
The coefficient form is obtained directly from
\begin{align*}
\pv{\Omega}
&=\frac{f\,\dfrac{\dd f}{\dd\lambda}
        -\vct{g}\cdot\dfrac{\dd\vct{g}}{\dd\lambda}}
        {f^2-\vct{g}^2}\\
&\quad+
\frac{f\,\dfrac{\dd\vct{g}}{\dd\lambda}
      -\dfrac{\dd f}{\dd\lambda}\vct{g}
      -i\,\vct{g}\times\dfrac{\dd\vct{g}}{\dd\lambda}}
     {f^2-\vct{g}^2}\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
Relative to the primed basis, its vector coefficient is written uniquely as
\[
\pv{\Omega}
=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\Omega})
 +\tfrac12(\vct{k}+i\vct{\boldsymbol\omega})\cdot\sigv',
\qquad \vct{k},\vct{\boldsymbol\omega}\in\R^3.
\]
The same generator carried into the unprimed basis is defined by
\begin{equation}
\pv{\Omega}':=\pv{T}\pv{\Omega}\pv{T}^{-1}.
\label{eq:carried-similarity-generator}
\end{equation}
The definition in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:similarity-generator} then gives
\[
\pv{\Omega}'=\frac{\dd\pv{T}}{\dd\lambda}\pv{T}^{-1}.
\]
The coefficients are recovered through scalar extraction.
For \(n\in\{1,2,3\}\), the coefficients are given by cyclicity of the
algebraic scalar part:
\[
k_n+i\omega_n
=2\,\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\Omega}\pv{\sigma}_n')
=2\,\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\Omega}'\pv{\sigma}_n).
\]
The scalar part is central with respect to the basis, while
\begin{align*}
\frac{\dd\sigv'}{\dd\lambda}
&=(-\vct{\boldsymbol\omega}+i\vct{k})\times\sigv',\\
\frac{\dd\pv{Z}}{\dd\lambda}
&=\frac{\dd S}{\dd\lambda}
 +\frac{\dd\vct{V}}{\dd\lambda}\cdot\sigv'\notag\\
&\quad-\bigl(\vct{V}\times\vct{\boldsymbol\omega}\bigr)\cdot\sigv'\notag\\
&\quad+i\bigl(\vct{V}\times\vct{k}\bigr)\cdot\sigv'.
\end{align*}
These identities describe a basis that depends on \(\lambda\), and are
therefore called moving-basis identities. Similarity alone does not define
\(\lambda\) as space-time, energy, or proper time. Such an interpretation
requires a separately specified event law of the form
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:general-event-congruence} and a definition relating the
changing basis to a physical reference frame.

\subsubsection{Rotation}
Equation \eqref{eq:rotor} is obtained as the rotation case of the general
transform law by specializing the transform element to \(\pv{T}=\pv{R}\).
Sigma number two is assigned to the generator, so the exponential is circular
rather than hyperbolic. The rotor function defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:rotor-element} has the expanded form
\begin{align*}
\pv{R}(\theta ,\vct{u}) &= \exp( \tfrac{1}{2} \theta (\vct{u} \cdot i \sigv ) ),\notag \\
&= \cos( \tfrac{1}{2} \theta ) + \sin( \tfrac{1}{2} \theta ) (\vct{u} \cdot i \sigv ).
\end{align*}
The inverse is obtained as
\begin{equation*}
\pv{R}^{-1} = \exp( -\tfrac{1}{2} \theta (\vct{u} \cdot i \sigv ) ).
\end{equation*}
\(\theta\in\R\), and \(\vct{u}\in\R^3\) is a unit real vector. The element
\(\pv{R}\) is generated by the sigma-number-two element
\(\vct{u}\cdot i\sigv\). The
inverse relation is
\(\pv{R}^{-1}=\pv{R}^{\Hop}=\pv{R}^{*\Hop}\).
The sign of the \(\vct{u}\times\vct{r}\) term, and therefore the positive
rotation direction, is fixed by the substitution
\begin{equation*}
f=\cos(\theta/2),
\qquad
\vct{g}=i\sin(\theta/2)\vct{u}
\end{equation*}
into Eq.\ \eqref{eq:similarity-components}. Then
\begin{align*}
f^2-\vct{g}^2&=1,\\
f^2+\vct{g}^2&=\cos(\theta),\\
-2(\vct{g}\cdot\vct{r})\vct{g}
&=(1-\cos(\theta))(\vct{u}\cdot\vct{r})\vct{u},\\
-2i\,f(\vct{g}\times\vct{r})
&=\sin(\theta)(\vct{u}\times\vct{r}).
\end{align*}
Therefore the transformed paravector is
\begin{align*}
\pv{X}' &= \pv{R}^{\Hop} \pv{X} \pv{R},\notag \\
&= t' + \vct{r}' \cdot \sigv.
\end{align*}
The scalar and vector parts are therefore obtained as
\begin{align*}
t' &= t,\notag \\
\vct{r}' &= \vct{r} \cos(\theta ) + (\vct{u} \times \vct{r}) \sin(\theta ),\notag \\
&\quad + (\vct{u} \cdot \vct{r}) (1 - \cos(\theta )) \vct{u}.
\end{align*}
The vector formula is the standard axis--angle expression conventionally
called Rodrigues's formula \cite{doran2003}.
For a parameter-dependent rotor, the specialization of the general generator
is \(\pv{R}^{\Hop}(\dd\pv{R}/\dd\lambda)\). Its form carried to the
other side is
\((\dd\pv{R}/\dd\lambda)\pv{R}^{\Hop}\).
The following identities are obtained by differentiating
\(\pv{R}^{\Hop}\pv{R}=1\):
\[
\left(\pv{R}^{\Hop}\frac{\dd\pv{R}}{\dd\lambda}\right)^{\Hop}
=-\pv{R}^{\Hop}\frac{\dd\pv{R}}{\dd\lambda},
\qquad
\left(\frac{\dd\pv{R}}{\dd\lambda}\pv{R}^{\Hop}\right)^{\Hop}
=-\frac{\dd\pv{R}}{\dd\lambda}\pv{R}^{\Hop}.
\]
The rotation transport law is the specialization of the general
one-parameter similarity formula:
\begin{align*}
\frac{\dd\pv{X}'}{\dd\lambda}
&=\pv{R}^{\Hop}\frac{\dd\pv{X}}{\dd\lambda}\pv{R}
+\com{\pv{X}'}{\pv{R}^{\Hop}\dfrac{\dd\pv{R}}{\dd\lambda}}\\
&=\pv{R}^{\Hop}
\left(\frac{\dd\pv{X}}{\dd\lambda}
+\com{\pv{X}}{\dfrac{\dd\pv{R}}{\dd\lambda}\pv{R}^{\Hop}}\right)\pv{R}.
\end{align*}

\subsubsection{Spin Rotors}
Each oriented product of two different sigma generators is identified by
the pseudoscalar without an index-order ambiguity:
\[
i\pv{\sigma}_1=\pv{\sigma}_2\pv{\sigma}_3,\qquad
i\pv{\sigma}_2=\pv{\sigma}_3\pv{\sigma}_1,\qquad
i\pv{\sigma}_3=\pv{\sigma}_1\pv{\sigma}_2.
\]
Such a two-generator product is the sigma-number-two element already
defined above; it is conventionally called a bivector.
For unit \(\vct{n},\vct{u}\in\R^3\), the rotor function defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:rotor-element} is evaluated as
\begin{align*}
\pv{R}(\phi,\vct{n})
&=\exp\!\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\,\vct{n}\cdot i\sigv\right)\\
&=\cos(\phi/2)
 +\sin(\phi/2)\,\vct{n}\cdot i\sigv .
\end{align*}
Then
\[
\pv{R}^{-1}=\pv{R}^{\Hop}=\pv{R}^{*\Hop},\qquad
\pv{R}^{\Hop}\pv{R}=1,
\]
and
\[
\pv{R}^{\Hop}(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)\pv{R}
=\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv
\]
with \(\vct{u}'\) defined by Rodrigues's formula. The rotor itself is
single-valued and \(4\pi\)-periodic:
\[
\pv{R}(\phi+2\pi,\vct{n})=-\pv{R}(\phi,\vct{n}),\qquad
\pv{R}(\phi+4\pi,\vct{n})=\pv{R}(\phi,\vct{n}).
\]
The induced spatial rotation is two-to-one because the same conjugation is
produced by \(\pv{R}\) and \(-\pv{R}\).

Under the active convention associated with this vector action, an algebra
input and its opposite-order product transform as
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'
&=\pv{R}^{\Hop}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}},\\
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}'^{\Hop}
&=\pv{R}^{\Hop}\notag\\
&\quad{}
\bigl(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{algebra\_input}}^{\Hop}\bigr)\pv{R}.
\end{align*}
A physical spin state additionally requires a scalar rule for pairing two
states, a normalization, one fixed right-projector restriction, a Hermitian
element representing the measurement, and a rule for its predicted average.
Those choices are defined in
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:adjoint-paravector-braket} and
\eqref{eq:expectation-map}. The algebra action is thereby separated from
the choice of a single physical spin state.
