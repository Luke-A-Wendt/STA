\subsubsection{Projector Products and Ladder Operations}
The projector family in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projectors} and the ladder elements in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:ladder} admit the following detailed identities:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\Pi(\pm)^2&=\Pi(\pm),\qquad
\Pi(+)+\Pi(-)=1,\\
(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)\Pi(\pm)&=\pm\Pi(\pm).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
with
\begin{equation*}
\Pi(\pm)^{\mathrm R}=\Pi(\pm),
\qquad
\Pi(\pm)^*=\Pi(\mp).
\end{equation*}
For a second unit direction \(\mathbf u'\), the same-side and cross-side products are
\begin{equation*}
\Pi(\pm)(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)\Pi(\pm)=\pm(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u')\Pi(\pm),
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\Pi(\pm)(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)\Pi(\mp)
=\frac12\Bigl(\mathbf u'_{\perp}\pm i\,\mathbf u\times\mathbf u'\Bigr)\cdot\sigv\,\Pi(\mp).
\end{equation*}
If \(\theta\) is the angle between the two axes, then
\begin{equation*}
\frac12(1+\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u')=\cos^{2}\frac{\theta}{2},
\qquad
\frac12(1-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u')=\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}.
\end{equation*}
These are the projector identities behind the standard half-angle transition factors.

The ladder elements exchange the two projector parts selected by Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projectors}. They satisfy the sharper one-sided identities
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
a(+)\Pi(+) &= 0, &
a(+)\Pi(-) &= a(+), \\
\Pi(+)a(+) &= a(+), &
\Pi(-)a(+) &= 0, \\
a(-)\Pi(-) &= 0, &
a(-)\Pi(+) &= a(-), \\
\Pi(-)a(-) &= a(-), &
\Pi(+)a(-) &= 0,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
which make the raising/lowering interpretation explicit. The original sigma generators are recovered from the same ladder/projector data:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_1&=a(+)+a(-),\qquad
\sigma_2=\frac{a(+)-a(-)}{i},\\
\sigma_3&=\Pi(+)-\Pi(-).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

\subsubsection{Commutators of Unit-Direction Elements}
For unit directions \(\mathbf u,\mathbf u'\in\R^3\),
\begin{equation*}
(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)=\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u'+i(\mathbf u\times\mathbf u')\cdot\sigv,
\end{equation*}
so the commutator is
\begin{equation*}
[\mathbf u\cdot\sigv,\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv]=2i(\mathbf u\times\mathbf u')\cdot\sigv.
\end{equation*}
Its norm is controlled by the angle between the axes,
\begin{equation*}
\left\|\frac12[\mathbf u\cdot\sigv,\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv]\right\|^{2}
=(\mathbf u\times\mathbf u')^{2}.
\end{equation*}
This is the algebraic content behind axis noncommutativity. It is an operator-level identity for the algebra elements themselves. No probabilistic or variational interpretation is assumed here.
