\subsubsection{Spin-Axis Projectors}
A nonzero real axis vector \(\vct{A}\) is chosen first, and its unit
direction is
\[
\vct{u}:=\frac{\vct{A}}{\|\vct{A}\|}\in\R^3,
\qquad
\vct{u}^{\,2}=1,
\]
and its left action is resolved by the projector family from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projectors}. For clarity, that definition is restated here
as an ordinary equality:
\[
\pv{\Pi}(\pm)=\tfrac12(1\pm\vct{u}\cdot\sigv),
\qquad
\text{from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projectors}.}
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:projectors} also shows that each projector is unchanged
when squared, their mixed products are zero, their sum is \(1\), and
multiplication by \(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv\) returns each projector multiplied by
its sign. The following star and \(\Hop\) actions are obtained:
\begin{align*}
\pv{\Pi}(\pm)^{\Hop}&=\pv{\Pi}(\pm),\\
\pv{\Pi}(+)^*&=\pv{\Pi}(-),\\
\pv{\Pi}(-)^*&=\pv{\Pi}(+).
\end{align*}
Accordingly,
\begin{equation*}
\vct{u}\cdot\sigv=(+1)\pv{\Pi}(+)+(-1)\pv{\Pi}(-),
\end{equation*}
so the eigenvalues \(+1\) and \(-1\) of the axis element are carried by the
two projector parts. Here an eigenvalue is the scalar returned when the
action leaves a nonzero projector part otherwise unchanged.

These left projectors split any algebra element into the two axis parts
defined in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projected-part-function}. A later physical
two-amplitude wave field is additionally restricted by one fixed projector
on the right. The left axis split and the fixed right restriction have
different roles and must not be identified.

If the axis is rotated by the rotor \(\pv{R}\) from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:rotor},
the transformed axis and its second projector pair satisfy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv&=\herm{\pv{R}}(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)\pv{R},
\qquad
\vct{u}'{}^2=1,
\\
\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)&=\tfrac12(1\pm \vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)
=\herm{\pv{R}}\pv{\Pi}(\pm)\pv{R}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:rotated-axis-projectors}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation*}
\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv=(+1)\pv{\Pi}'(+)+(-1)\pv{\Pi}'(-).
\end{equation*}

For an axis state
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}\in\CPspace\), the left-projected parts
from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projectors} are
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(\pm)
=\pv{\Pi}(\pm)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}.
\end{equation*}
The following identities are obtained:
\begin{align*}
(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(\pm)
&=\pm\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(\pm),\\
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}
&=\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(+)
+\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(-).
\end{align*}
Thus each Pauli state is split into two parts on which the chosen axis acts
by multiplication by \(+1\) or \(-1\). The sets of states with these fixed
multipliers are conventionally called eigenspaces
\cite{pauli1927,sakurai2017}.
The Hermitian operation is represented by
\begin{align*}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(\pm)}
&=\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}\,
\pv{\Pi}(\pm)^{\Hop}\notag\\
&=\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}\,\pv{\Pi}(\pm),
\end{align*}
and therefore
\begin{align*}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(\pm)}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(\pm)
&=\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}\notag\\
&\quad{}
\pv{\Pi}(\pm)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}},\\
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(+)}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(-)
&=\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}\notag\\
&\quad{}
\pv{\Pi}(+)\pv{\Pi}(-)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}=0,\\
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(-)}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(+)
&=\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}\notag\\
&\quad{}
\pv{\Pi}(-)\pv{\Pi}(+)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}=0.
\end{align*}
For the axis states
\(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}\) and
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}\), and a specified measurement element
\(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\), the algebraic pairing at one position is
\(\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}})\).
For a nonzero \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}\), the identity pairing
\(\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}})\) is positive by
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:euclidean-form}. Division by this positive value gives
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}})}
{\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}})}.
\end{equation*}
This quotient is a normalized operator pairing because the same state occurs
on both sides of \(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\). When
\(\herm{\pv{\mathcal{O}}}=\pv{\mathcal{O}}\), the numerator is real. If
\(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\) represents a
measurement in quantum mechanics, the quotient is its predicted average,
conventionally called its expectation value \cite{sakurai2017}. For a
general \(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\), it is only a normalized pairing and may be
complex.
The common scalar-extraction factor cancels in the normalized pairing. The
corresponding spatially integrated pairing is defined separately in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:adjoint-paravector-braket}.

An active rotation means that the state and measurement element are rotated
while the coordinate axes are held fixed. Under this convention,
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}'
=\herm{\pv{R}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}},
\qquad
\pv{\mathcal{O}}'=\herm{\pv{R}}\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{R},
\qquad
\pv{R}\herm{\pv{R}}=1,
\end{equation*}
both the numerator and denominator of the normalized pairing are
unchanged:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}'}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}'\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}')}
{\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}'}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}')}
=
\frac{\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}})}
{\eqfunc{scalar}(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}})}.
\label{eq:rotor-expectation-covariance}
\end{equation}
The unchanged form of this value under a common rotation is called
rotational covariance. Equivalently, if the Hermitian measurement element
\(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\), conventionally called an observable, is held fixed while only
the state is actively rotated, its value in the original state is computed
with the rotated-back observable
\(\pv{R}\pv{\mathcal{O}}\herm{\pv{R}}\). Describing
the same change by rotating the axes while holding the state fixed is called
the passive description. This active-versus-passive equivalence is expressed in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:rotated-axis-projectors}.

The Hermitian element \(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv\) represents measurement along
the chosen axis and is therefore an axis observable. The following identity
is obtained by cyclic scalar extraction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
  \herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
  (\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}
\right)\\
&\quad=
\vct{u}\cdot
\eqfunc{vector}\!\left(
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:axis-polarization-identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently its normalized value at one position is
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\vct{u}\cdot
  \eqfunc{vector}(
  \pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}
  \herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}})}
 {\eqfunc{scalar}(
 \herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}
 \pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}})}.
\end{equation*}
For a spatial wave field, the same statement is valid with both quantities
integrated as in
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:adjoint-paravector-braket} and \eqref{eq:expectation-map}.

The unnormalized numerator at one position for the rotated axis is expanded as
\begin{align*}
&\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}}\\[-0.2em]
&\quad{}(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}
\end{aligned}
\right)\notag\\
&\quad=\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(+)}\\[-0.2em]
&\quad{}(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(+)
\end{aligned}
\right)\notag\\
&\qquad+\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(+)}\\[-0.2em]
&\quad{}(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(-)
\end{aligned}
\right)\notag\\
&\qquad+\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(-)}\\[-0.2em]
&\quad{}(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(+)
\end{aligned}
\right)\notag\\
&\qquad+\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(-)}\\[-0.2em]
&\quad{}(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{axis\_state}}(-)
\end{aligned}
\right).
\end{align*}

The algebra governing these same-side and cross-side terms is
\begin{align*}
(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)
&=\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u}+i(\vct{u}'\times\vct{u})\cdot\sigv,\\
(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)
&=\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u}-i(\vct{u}'\times\vct{u})\cdot\sigv,\\
(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)
&=\bigl(2(\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u})\vct{u}-\vct{u}'\bigr)\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
The second axis is decomposed into its \(\eqfunc{par}\) and
\(\eqfunc{prp}\) parts by the axis functions from
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:par-function} and \eqref{eq:prp-function}:
\begin{equation*}
\vct{u}'=\eqfunc{par}(\vct{u}')+\eqfunc{prp}(\vct{u}'),
\qquad
\eqfunc{par}(\vct{u}')=(\vct{u}\cdot\vct{u}')\vct{u}.
\end{equation*}
Then the projector products are
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\Pi}(\pm)(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\Pi}(\pm)=\pm(\vct{u}\cdot\vct{u}')\pv{\Pi}(\pm).
\end{equation*}
The off-diagonal products are obtained as
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\pv{\Pi}(+)(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\Pi}(-)
&=\frac12\Bigl(\eqfunc{prp}(\vct{u}')
+i\,\vct{u}\times\vct{u}'\Bigr)\cdot\sigv\,\pv{\Pi}(-),\\
\pv{\Pi}(-)(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\Pi}(+)
&=\frac12\Bigl(\eqfunc{prp}(\vct{u}')
-i\,\vct{u}\times\vct{u}'\Bigr)\cdot\sigv\,\pv{\Pi}(+).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The diagonal terms are therefore weighted by the axis overlap
\(\vct{u}\cdot\vct{u}'\), while the off-diagonal terms are controlled by the
\(\eqfunc{prp}\) and commutator parts of the rotated axis.

If \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\vct{u}\) and \(\vct{u}'\), then
\begin{equation*}
\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u}=\cos(\theta).
\end{equation*}
The normalized projector overlap is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\!\bigl(\pv{\Pi}(\pm)\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\bigr)
&=\frac12\bigl(1+\vct{u}\cdot\vct{u}'\bigr),\\
2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\!\bigl(\pv{\Pi}(+)\pv{\Pi}'(-)\bigr)
&=2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\!\bigl(\pv{\Pi}(-)\pv{\Pi}'(+)\bigr)\notag\\
&=\frac12\bigl(1-\vct{u}\cdot\vct{u}'\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
When a normalized two-amplitude state lies entirely in one projector part,
these overlaps give the probabilities for the same-sign and opposite-sign
parts along the second axis. This predicted change from one selected axis
part to another is called a transition probability
\cite{sakurai2017}. In particular, the same-side and opposite-side
probabilities are
\begin{equation*}
\frac12(1+\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u})=\cos^2(\theta/2),
\qquad
\frac12(1-\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u})=\sin^2(\theta/2).
\end{equation*}
If \(\vct{u}'\) is obtained by rotating \(\vct{u}\) through angle \(\phi\)
about a unit axis \(\vct{n}\), the overlap is given by the standard
axis--angle formula commonly called Rodrigues's formula
\cite{doran2003}:
\begin{align*}
\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u}
&=(\vct{u}\cdot\vct{u})\cos(\phi)
+\bigl(\vct{u}\cdot(\vct{n}\times\vct{u})\bigr)\sin(\phi)\notag\\
&\quad+(\vct{n}\cdot\vct{u})\bigl(1-\cos(\phi)\bigr)(\vct{n}\cdot\vct{u})\notag\\
&=(\vct{n}\cdot\vct{u})^2+\bigl(1-(\vct{n}\cdot\vct{u})^2\bigr)\cos(\phi).
\end{align*}
In the perpendicular case \(\vct{n}\cdot\vct{u}=0\), this is reduced to
\begin{equation*}
\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u}=\cos(\phi),
\end{equation*}
hence
\begin{equation*}
\frac12(1+\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u})=\cos^2(\phi/2),
\qquad
\frac12(1-\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{u})=\sin^2(\phi/2).
\end{equation*}
These are the standard transition probabilities for a two-outcome Pauli
spin state, conventionally called a spin-\(\tfrac12\) state
\cite{pauli1927,sakurai2017}. Without normalization and the later
two-amplitude right-projector restriction, only algebraic projector overlaps
are represented.

\subsubsection{Ladder Operations About the z Axis}
For raising and lowering about the \(z\) axis, the following axis and
projector pair are chosen:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\vct{u}&=\vct{e}_3=\vthree{0}{0}{1},
\qquad
\vct{u}^2=1,
\\
\pv{\Pi}(\pm)&
=\tfrac12(1\pm\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)
=\tfrac12(1\pm \pv{\sigma}_3).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The ladder elements already defined in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:ladder} are used:
\begin{equation*}
\pv{a}(\pm)=\tfrac12(\pv{\sigma}_1\pm i\pv{\sigma}_2).
\end{equation*}
The commutator relation is satisfied:
\begin{equation*}
\com{\pv{\sigma}_3}{\pv{a}(\pm)}=\pm 2\pv{a}(\pm),
\end{equation*}
so the \(\pv{\sigma}_3\) eigenvalue is raised by \(\pv{a}(+)\) and lowered
by \(\pv{a}(-)\).

Their projector products are
\begin{equation*}
\pv{a}(+)\pv{a}(-)=\pv{\Pi}(+),
\qquad
\pv{a}(-)\pv{a}(+)=\pv{\Pi}(-),
\end{equation*}
and each ladder element has square zero:
\begin{equation*}
\pv{a}(\pm)^2=0.
\end{equation*}
An element for which a positive power is zero is conventionally called
nilpotent; here that power is \(2\).
The one-sided projector actions are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\pv{a}(+)\pv{\Pi}(+) &= 0, &
\pv{a}(+)\pv{\Pi}(-) &= \pv{a}(+), \\
\pv{\Pi}(+)\pv{a}(+) &= \pv{a}(+), &
\pv{\Pi}(-)\pv{a}(+) &= 0, \\
\pv{a}(-)\pv{\Pi}(-) &= 0, &
\pv{a}(-)\pv{\Pi}(+) &= \pv{a}(-), \\
\pv{\Pi}(-)\pv{a}(-) &= \pv{a}(-), &
\pv{\Pi}(+)\pv{a}(-) &= 0,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
by which the raising/lowering interpretation is made explicit. The original
sigma generators are recovered from the same ladder/projector data:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\pv{\sigma}_1&=\pv{a}(+)+\pv{a}(-),\qquad
\pv{\sigma}_2=\frac{\pv{a}(+)-\pv{a}(-)}{i},\\
\pv{\sigma}_3&=\pv{\Pi}(+)-\pv{\Pi}(-).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

\subsubsection{Spin-Axis Noncommutativity}
For unit directions \(\vct{u},\vct{u}'\in\R^3\),
\begin{align*}
\acom{\vct{u}\cdot\sigv}{\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv}
&=2\,\vct{u}\cdot\vct{u}',\\
\com{\vct{u}\cdot\sigv}{\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv}
&=2i(\vct{u}\times\vct{u}')\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
The axis overlap is recorded by the anticommutator, while the oriented area
spanned by the two axes is recorded by the commutator. Thus exact commutation
of the axis elements is obtained when the directions are parallel or
antiparallel. These identities are purely algebraic. A normalized state,
a rule for calculating measurement averages, and each measurement's mean
squared deviation from its average are additionally required. The last
quantity is called the variance, and its square root is called the
uncertainty. Only after these choices are supplied can
the commutator be used in the standard inequality that bounds the product
of two measurement uncertainties, called the Robertson or
Robertson--Schr\"odinger uncertainty relation. No such inequality is
inferred from the commutator alone \cite{robertson1929}.
