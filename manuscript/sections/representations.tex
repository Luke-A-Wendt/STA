\subsubsection{Spin-Axis Projectors}
The projector family from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projectors} resolves the left
action of a chosen unit axis \(\mathbf u\in\R^3\). The basic identities are
\begin{align*}
\Pi(\pm)&=\tfrac12(1\pm \mathbf u\cdot\sigv),\\
\Pi(\pm)^2&=\Pi(\pm),\\
\Pi(+)\Pi(-)&=0,\\
\Pi(+)+\Pi(-)&=1,\\
(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)\Pi(\pm)&=\pm\Pi(\pm),\\
\Pi(\pm)^{\mathrm R}&=\Pi(\pm),\\
\Pi(\pm)^*&=\Pi(\mp).
\end{align*}
Accordingly,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf u\cdot\sigv=(+1)\Pi(+)+(-1)\Pi(-),
\end{equation*}
so the two projector parts carry the eigenvalues \(+1\) and \(-1\) of the axis operator.

In the Pauli matrix realization, each \(\Pi(\pm)\) has rank one. A general
element of \(\C\oplus\C^3\), however, is a full \(2\times2\) complex matrix,
not one irreducible two-component spinor. The following projection formulas
therefore hold column by column. They describe a single Pauli spinor after
one fixes a minimal left ideal by placing a fixed projector on the right;
without that restriction, the full
paravector describes two spinor columns or may be interpreted as a density
amplitude.

If the axis is rotated by the rotor \(R\) from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:rotor}, define
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv=\rev{R}(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)R,
\qquad
\Pi'(\pm):=\tfrac12(1\pm \mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)
=\rev{R}\Pi(\pm)R.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv=(+1)\Pi'(+)+(-1)\Pi'(-).
\end{equation*}

For an algebra element \(\psi\in\C\oplus\C^3\), define its left-projected parts
\begin{equation*}
\psi(\pm):=\Pi(\pm)\psi.
\end{equation*}
These satisfy
\begin{align*}
(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)\psi(\pm)&=\pm\psi(\pm),\\
\psi&=\psi(+)+\psi(-).
\end{align*}
Thus each spinor column splits into the two eigenspaces of the chosen axis. Reversion acts as
\begin{align*}
\rev{\psi(\pm)}
&=\rev{\psi}\,\Pi(\pm)^{\mathrm R}\notag\\
&=\rev{\psi}\,\Pi(\pm),
\end{align*}
and therefore
\begin{align*}
\rev{\psi(\pm)}\psi(\pm)&=\rev{\psi}\,\Pi(\pm)\psi,\\
\rev{\psi(\pm)}\psi(\mp)&=\rev{\psi}\,\Pi(\pm)\Pi(\mp)\psi=0,\\
\rev{\psi(\mp)}\psi(\pm)&=\rev{\psi}\,\Pi(\mp)\Pi(\pm)\psi=0.
\end{align*}
For two algebra elements \(\phi,\psi\), define the mixed pairing
\begin{equation*}
\langle\phi\mid M\mid\psi\rangle
:=\eqtext{scalar}(\rev{\phi}M\psi).
\end{equation*}
For a nonzero \(\psi\), an expectation value is the normalized ratio
\begin{equation*}
\langle M\rangle_\psi
:=\frac{\langle\psi\mid M\mid\psi\rangle}
        {\eqtext{scalar}(\rev{\psi}\psi)}.
\end{equation*}
For a full matrix \(\psi\), this is the Hilbert--Schmidt or density-amplitude
pairing; for a vector in a fixed minimal left ideal, it is the ordinary
Pauli-spinor pairing. The unnormalized numerator for the rotated axis
expands as
\begin{align*}
\langle\psi\mid \mathbf u'\cdot\sigv \mid \psi\rangle
&=\langle\psi(+)+\psi(-)\mid \mathbf u'\cdot\sigv \mid \psi(+)+\psi(-)\rangle\notag\\
&=\langle\psi(+)\mid \mathbf u'\cdot\sigv \mid \psi(+)\rangle\notag\\
&\quad+\langle\psi(+)\mid \mathbf u'\cdot\sigv \mid \psi(-)\rangle\notag\\
&\quad+\langle\psi(-)\mid \mathbf u'\cdot\sigv \mid \psi(+)\rangle\notag\\
&\quad+\langle\psi(-)\mid \mathbf u'\cdot\sigv \mid \psi(-)\rangle.
\end{align*}

The algebra governing these same-side and cross-side terms is
\begin{align*}
(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)
&=\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u+i(\mathbf u'\times\mathbf u)\cdot\sigv,\\
(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)
&=\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u-i(\mathbf u'\times\mathbf u)\cdot\sigv,\\
(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)
&=\bigl(2(\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u)\mathbf u-\mathbf u'\bigr)\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
Write the decomposition of the second axis into parts parallel and perpendicular to \(\mathbf u\) as
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf u'_{\parallel}:=(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u')\mathbf u,
\qquad
\mathbf u'_{\perp}:=\mathbf u'-\mathbf u'_{\parallel}.
\end{equation*}
Then the projector products are
\begin{equation*}
\Pi(\pm)(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)\Pi(\pm)=\pm(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u')\Pi(\pm),
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\Pi(\pm)(\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv)\Pi(\mp)
=\frac12\Bigl(\mathbf u'_{\perp}\pm i\,\mathbf u\times\mathbf u'\Bigr)\cdot\sigv\,\Pi(\mp).
\end{equation*}
The diagonal terms are therefore weighted by the axis overlap \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u'\), while the off-diagonal terms are controlled by the perpendicular and commutator parts of the rotated axis.

If \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf u'\), then
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u=\cos(\theta),
\end{equation*}
and the rank-one projector overlap is
\begin{equation*}
2\,\eqtext{scalar}\!\bigl(\Pi(s)\Pi'(s')\bigr)
=\frac12\bigl(1+ss'\,\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u'\bigr),
\qquad s,s'\in\{+1,-1\}.
\end{equation*}
For a normalized irreducible axis eigenstate this overlap is the transition
probability. In particular, the same-side and opposite-side probabilities
are
\begin{equation*}
\frac12(1+\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u)=\cos^2(\theta/2),
\qquad
\frac12(1-\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u)=\sin^2(\theta/2).
\end{equation*}
If \(\mathbf u'\) is obtained by rotating \(\mathbf u\) through angle \(\phi\) about a unit axis \(\mathbf n\), Rodrigues's formula gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u
&=(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u)\cos(\phi)
+\bigl(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf n\times\mathbf u)\bigr)\sin(\phi)\notag\\
&\quad+(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u)\bigl(1-\cos(\phi)\bigr)(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u)\notag\\
&=(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u)^2+\bigl(1-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u)^2\bigr)\cos(\phi).
\end{align*}
In the perpendicular case \(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u=0\), this reduces to
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u=\cos(\phi),
\end{equation*}
hence
\begin{equation*}
\frac12(1+\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u)=\cos^2(\phi/2),
\qquad
\frac12(1-\mathbf u'\cdot\mathbf u)=\sin^2(\phi/2).
\end{equation*}
These are the familiar spin-\(\tfrac12\) transition probabilities. Without
the normalization and irreducible-state qualification above, they should
be read only as projector overlaps.

\subsubsection{Ladder Operations About the z Axis}
For raising and lowering about the \(z\) axis, choose
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf u=\mathbf e_3=(0,0,1),
\qquad
\Pi(\pm)=\tfrac12(1\pm \sigma_3).
\end{equation*}
Define the ladder operators by
\begin{equation*}
\ell(\pm):=\tfrac12(\sigma_1\pm i\sigma_2).
\end{equation*}
They satisfy the commutator relation
\begin{equation*}
\com{\sigma_3}{\ell(\pm)}=\pm 2\ell(\pm),
\end{equation*}
so \(\ell(+)\) raises the \(\sigma_3\) eigenvalue and \(\ell(-)\) lowers it.

Their projector products are
\begin{equation*}
\ell(+)\ell(-)=\Pi(+),
\qquad
\ell(-)\ell(+)=\Pi(-),
\end{equation*}
and each ladder operator is nilpotent:
\begin{equation*}
\ell(\pm)^2=0.
\end{equation*}
The one-sided projector actions are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\ell(+)\Pi(+) &= 0, &
\ell(+)\Pi(-) &= \ell(+), \\
\Pi(+)\ell(+) &= \ell(+), &
\Pi(-)\ell(+) &= 0, \\
\ell(-)\Pi(-) &= 0, &
\ell(-)\Pi(+) &= \ell(-), \\
\Pi(-)\ell(-) &= \ell(-), &
\Pi(+)\ell(-) &= 0,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
which make the raising/lowering interpretation explicit. The original sigma generators are recovered from the same ladder/projector data:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_1&=\ell(+)+\ell(-),\qquad
\sigma_2=\frac{\ell(+)-\ell(-)}{i},\\
\sigma_3&=\Pi(+)-\Pi(-).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

\subsubsection{Spin-Axis Noncommutativity}
For unit directions \(\mathbf u,\mathbf u'\in\R^3\),
\begin{align*}
\acom{\mathbf u\cdot\sigv}{\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv}
&=2\,\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u',\\
\com{\mathbf u\cdot\sigv}{\mathbf u'\cdot\sigv}
&=2i(\mathbf u\times\mathbf u')\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
The anticommutator records the axis overlap, while the commutator records
the oriented area spanned by the two axes. Thus the axis operators commute
exactly when the directions are parallel or antiparallel. These identities
are purely algebraic. A Robertson or Robertson--Schr\"odinger uncertainty
relation additionally requires a normalized state, expectation functional,
and state-dependent variances; no such inequality is inferred from the
commutator alone \cite{robertson1929}.
