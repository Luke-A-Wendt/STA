\subsubsection{Real Exponential and Half-Angle Elements}
With the projector calculus in hand, the sigma-number-one exponential becomes especially transparent. For the real paravector
\[
X=a+\mathbf A\cdot\sigv\in\R\oplus\R^3,
\]
write
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf A&=\theta \mathbf u,\qquad \theta=\lVert \mathbf A\rVert,\\
\mathbf u&=\frac{\mathbf A}{\lVert \mathbf A\rVert}\ \ (\mathbf A\neq 0),\qquad
\mathbf u=(u_1,u_2,u_3),\\
\mathbf u^2&=1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Then Eq.\ \eqref{eq:functional-calculus} gives
\begin{align}
\exp(\pm\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)
&= \exp(\pm\theta\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)\notag \\
&= \exp(\pm\theta)\Pi(+) + \exp(\mp\theta)\Pi(-)\notag \\
&= \cosh(\theta) \pm \sinh(\theta)\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:exp-vector}
\end{align}
Equivalently, the same result follows directly from the power series by separating the even and odd powers:
\begin{align*}
\exp(\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)
&= \exp(\theta\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)\notag \\
&= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^n(\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)^n}{n!}\notag \\
&= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{2n}}{(2n)!}
+ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv\notag \\
&= \cosh(\theta)+\sinh(\theta)\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
With
\begin{align*}
\gamma &= \cosh(\theta),\notag \\
\mathbf v &= \tanh(\theta)\,\mathbf u,
\end{align*}
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:exp-vector} becomes the basic hyperbolic parametrization used repeatedly below:
\begin{align*}
\exp(\mathbf A\cdot\sigv) &= \gamma(1+\mathbf v\cdot\sigv),\notag \\
\exp(-\mathbf A\cdot\sigv) &= \gamma(1-\mathbf v\cdot\sigv).
\end{align*}
For the real paravector \(X\) from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:real-paravector-x}, the scalar part commutes with the vector part, so
\begin{align*}
\exp(X) &= \exp(a)\exp(\mathbf A\cdot\sigv),\notag \\
\exp(X^{*}) &= \exp(a)\exp(-\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)=\exp(X)^{*},\notag \\
\exp(-X) &= \exp(-a)\exp(-\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)=\exp(X)^{-1}.
\end{align*}
The corresponding half-angle element is
\begin{equation}
L(\theta,\mathbf u)=\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv\right),
\qquad
X'=\conj{L}X\conj{L}.
\label{eq:boost}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:boost} is the \(T=L\) specialization of the general
event congruence \eqref{eq:general-event-congruence}, derived below from
the carrier transformation. Because \(L^{\mathsf R}=L\) and
\(L^*=L^{-1}\), it reads
\[
X'=L^{-1}X(L^{-1})^{\mathsf R}.
\]
It preserves the real-paravector subspace and mixes the scalar component
with the vector component parallel to \(\mathbf u\). It must be distinguished
from the similarity \(T^{-1}XT\), which preserves the scalar part and is
used below as an algebra and basis automorphism. For rotations the two
actions have the same appearance because the rotor is unitary; for
hyperbolic elements they do not.
Finally, the conjugate operator simply exchanges the two projector weights:
\begin{align*}
\exp(\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)^{*}
&= \exp(-\theta)\Pi(+) + \exp(\theta)\Pi(-)\notag \\
&= \gamma(1-\mathbf v\cdot\sigv)\notag \\
&= \exp(-\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)\notag \\
&= \exp\bigl((\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)^{*}\bigr)\notag \\
&= \exp(\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)^{-1}.
\end{align*}

\subsubsection{Real Logarithm of a Paravector}
The logarithm is the inverse problem for the same projector formulas. Let \(Y\) be the real paravector from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:real-paravector-y}, and assume \(b>\lVert\mathbf B\rVert\), so that the two real eigenvalues are positive and the principal logarithm is defined. For \(\mathbf B\neq0\), write
\(\mathbf B=\lVert\mathbf B\rVert \mathbf u\). Then
\begin{align*}
\lambda(\pm) &= b \pm \lVert\mathbf B\rVert,\notag \\
Y &= \lambda(+)\Pi(+) + \lambda(-)\Pi(-),
\end{align*}
with the same projector pair \(\Pi(\pm)=\tfrac12(1\pm \mathbf u\cdot\sigv)\). The logarithm therefore acts separately on the two eigenvalues:
\begin{align}
\log(Y)
&= \log\!\bigl(\lambda(+)\bigr)\Pi(+) + \log\!\bigl(\lambda(-)\bigr)\Pi(-)\notag \\
&= \tfrac12 \log\!\bigl(\lambda(+)\lambda(-)\bigr)
+ \tfrac12 \log\!\bigl(\lambda(+)/\lambda(-)\bigr)\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:log-paravector}
\end{align}
When \(\mathbf B=\mathbf0\), the axis is unnecessary and this formula
reduces directly to \(\log(Y)=\log(b)\).
For \(\mathbf B\neq0\), if \(Y=\exp(X)\) with \(X\) from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:real-paravector-x} and \(\mathbf A=\alpha\mathbf u\), then
\(\lambda(\pm)=\exp(a\pm\alpha)\), so the recovered parameters are
\begin{align*}
Y
&=\exp(X)\notag \\
&=\exp(a+\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)\notag \\
&=\exp(a)\exp(\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)\notag \\
&=\exp(a)\bigl(\cosh(\alpha)+\sinh(\alpha)\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv\bigr)\notag \\
&=b+\lVert\mathbf B\rVert\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv,
\end{align*}
so one reads off
\begin{align*}
\alpha &= \operatorname{arctanh}\!\bigl(\lVert\mathbf B\rVert/b\bigr), \\
\exp(a) &= \sqrt{b^{2}-\mathbf B^{2}}, \\
a &= \tfrac12 \log\!\bigl(b^{2}-\mathbf B^{2}\bigr), \\
\mathbf A &= \alpha\,\mathbf u
= \operatorname{arctanh}\!\bigl(\lVert\mathbf B\rVert/b\bigr)\,
\frac{\mathbf B}{\lVert\mathbf B\rVert}.
\end{align*}
Thus the principal logarithm of a future-directed time-like real paravector
is again a real paravector,
\begin{equation*}
\log(Y)=a+\mathbf A\cdot\sigv.
\end{equation*}

\subsubsection{Circular Exponentials and Half-Angle Elements}
Replacing \(\theta\) by \(i\theta\) turns the same projector calculation into the trigonometric form of the sigma-number-two exponential:
\begin{align}
\exp(\pm i\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)
&= \exp(\pm i\theta\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv)\notag \\
&= \exp(\pm i\theta)\Pi(+) + \exp(\mp i\theta)\Pi(-)\notag \\
&= \cos(\theta) \pm i\sin(\theta)\,\mathbf u\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:exp-bivector}
\end{align}
In particular, the negative-sign form may be written as
\begin{equation*}
\exp(-i\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)
=\cos(\theta)+\sin(\theta)\,\mathbf u\cdot(-i\sigv).
\end{equation*}
Later, after introducing the quaternion basis \(\mathbf q=-i\sigv\), this same formula becomes
\begin{equation*}
\exp(-i\mathbf A\cdot\sigv)
=\cos(\theta)+\sin(\theta)\,\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf q.
\end{equation*}
For a real angle \(\theta\in\R\) and a unit real vector \(\mathbf u\in\R^3\), the corresponding circular half-angle exponential is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R(\theta,\mathbf u)&:=\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\,\mathbf u\cdot i\sigv\right),\\
\rev{R}R&=1,\qquad
X'=\rev{R}XR.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:rotor}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:rotor} is the circular transform built from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:exp-bivector}. Because \(R^*=R\), it is simultaneously the
\(T=R\) specialization of the general similarity
\eqref{eq:general-transform} and of the event congruence
\eqref{eq:general-event-congruence}. Their common derivation and the detailed
transform consequences are given in the generalized transform section. The
quaternion restatement of the same sigma-number-two algebra is collected
later, after the transform discussion has been completed.

\subsubsection{Exponentials of Complex Paravectors}
Write the real paravectors
\begin{align*}
X &= a + \mathbf A \cdot \sigv \in \R\oplus\R^{3},\notag \\
Y &= b + \mathbf B \cdot \sigv \in \R\oplus\R^{3},\notag \\
Z &= S + \mathbf V \cdot \sigv \in \C\oplus\C^{3},\notag \\
&= X + i Y,
\end{align*}
where
\begin{equation*}
S:=a+ib\in\C,
\qquad
\mathbf V:=\mathbf A+i\mathbf B\in\C^3.
\end{equation*}
Because the scalar part \(S\) commutes with \(\mathbf V\cdot\sigv\), the exponential separates into scalar and directional factors:
\begin{align*}
\exp(Z) &= \exp( S + \mathbf V \cdot \sigv ),\notag \\
&= \exp(S) \exp( \mathbf V \cdot \sigv ).
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
\exp(Z^{*}) &= \exp(S^{*}) \exp( -\mathbf V^{*} \cdot \sigv ),\notag \\
\exp(Z)^{-1} &= \exp(-S) \exp( -\mathbf V \cdot \sigv ),\notag \\
\exp(Z^{*})^{-1} &= \exp(-S^{*}) \exp( \mathbf V^{*} \cdot \sigv ),\notag \\
(\mathbf V \cdot \sigv )^{2} &= \mathbf V \cdot \mathbf V = \mathbf V^{2},\notag \\
&= \mathbf A^{2} - \mathbf B^{2} + 2 i \mathbf A \cdot \mathbf B.
\end{align*}
The key simplification is that all higher powers collapse onto the two basis elements \(1\) and \(\mathbf V\cdot\sigv\):
\begin{align*}
(\mathbf V \cdot \sigv )^{2n} &= (\mathbf V^{2})^{n},\notag \\
z &= \sqrt{\mathbf V^{2}}\qquad \eqtext{complex scalar},\notag \\
\exp(\mathbf V \cdot \sigv ) &= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(\mathbf V \cdot \sigv )^{n}}{n!},\notag \\
&= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(\mathbf V^{2})^{n}}{(2n)!}
+ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(\mathbf V^{2})^{n}}{(2n+1)!}(\mathbf V \cdot \sigv ),\notag \\
&= \cosh(z) + \sinh(z) \frac{\mathbf V \cdot \sigv}{z}.
\end{align*}
The final expression is independent of the sign chosen for \(z\). When
\(\mathbf V^2=0\), the quotient \(\sinh(z)/z\) is understood by its
continuous value \(1\), and the formula becomes
\begin{equation*}
\exp(Z)=\exp(S)\bigl(1+\mathbf V\cdot\sigv\bigr).
\end{equation*}
