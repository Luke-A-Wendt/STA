\subsection{Standard Two-by-Two Complex Realization}
\label{sec:matrix-realization}
The sigma algebra is represented faithfully by two-by-two matrices with
complex scalar entries \cite{pauli1927,hestenes1966,baylis1999}. A
translation is called faithful when it is one-to-one and addition, complex
scalar multiplication, and the ordered product are preserved. The ordinary
matrix operations needed for that statement are defined first.

Two generic matrices are written as
\begin{equation}
\mat{M}:=
\begin{bmatrix}
m_{11}&m_{12}\\
m_{21}&m_{22}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mat{N}:=
\begin{bmatrix}
n_{11}&n_{12}\\
n_{21}&n_{22}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
m_{jk},n_{jk}\in\C,
\quad j,k\in\{1,2\}.
\label{eq:generic-two-by-two-matrices}
\end{equation}
Their ordered product is defined by
\begin{equation}
\mat{M}\mat{N}
:=
\begin{bmatrix}
m_{11}n_{11}+m_{12}n_{21}
&
m_{11}n_{12}+m_{12}n_{22}\\
m_{21}n_{11}+m_{22}n_{21}
&
m_{21}n_{12}+m_{22}n_{22}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:matrix-product}
\end{equation}
The multiplicative identity and trace are defined by
\begin{align}
\I
&:=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:matrix-identity}\\
\eqfunc{tr}(\mat{M})
&:=m_{11}+m_{22}.
\label{eq:matrix-trace}
\end{align}
Thus \(\I\mat{M}=\mat{M}\I=\mat{M}\), and the trace is the sum of the
diagonal entries.

The transpose is defined by
\begin{equation}
\mtrans{\mat{M}}
:=
\begin{bmatrix}
m_{11}&m_{21}\\
m_{12}&m_{22}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:matrix-transpose}
\end{equation}
For a complex scalar entry \(z=x+iy\), entrywise complex conjugation is
defined first by
\begin{equation}
\mconj{z}:=x-iy,
\qquad x,y\in\R,
\label{eq:matrix-entry-conjugate}
\end{equation}
and then on a matrix by
\begin{equation}
\mconj{\mat{M}}
:=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mconj{m_{11}}&\mconj{m_{12}}\\
\mconj{m_{21}}&\mconj{m_{22}}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:matrix-conjugate}
\end{equation}
The superscript \(\mathsf C\) is used only for matrices and their scalar
entries. The matrix Hermitian operation is defined by
\begin{equation}
\mherm{\mat{M}}
:=\mtrans{\mconj{\mat{M}}}
=\mconj{\mtrans{\mat{M}}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mconj{m_{11}}&\mconj{m_{21}}\\
\mconj{m_{12}}&\mconj{m_{22}}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:matrix-hermitian}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathsf H=\mathsf C\mathsf T=\mathsf T\mathsf C\) in ordinary
matrix notation.

The matrix adjugate and determinant are defined by
\begin{align}
\adjf{\mat{M}}
&:=
\begin{bmatrix}
m_{22}&-m_{12}\\
-m_{21}&m_{11}
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:matrix-adjugate}\\
\detf{\mat{M}}
&:=m_{11}m_{22}-m_{12}m_{21}.
\label{eq:matrix-determinant}
\end{align}
The following identity is obtained directly from the product in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:matrix-product}:
\begin{equation}
\mat{M}\adjf{\mat{M}}
=\adjf{\mat{M}}\mat{M}
=\detf{\mat{M}}\,\I.
\label{eq:matrix-adjugate-product}
\end{equation}
When \(\detf{\mat{M}}\neq0\), the multiplicative inverse is therefore
defined by
\begin{equation}
\mat{M}^{-1}
:=\frac{\adjf{\mat{M}}}{\detf{\mat{M}}},
\qquad
\mat{M}\mat{M}^{-1}
=\mat{M}^{-1}\mat{M}
=\I.
\label{eq:matrix-inverse}
\end{equation}

The three sigma generators are represented by the Pauli matrices
\begin{equation}
\mat{\sigma}_1=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mat{\sigma}_2=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&-i\\
i&0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mat{\sigma}_3=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:pauli-matrices}
\end{equation}
The same defining rule as for the intrinsic generators is satisfied by
their matrix products:
\begin{equation}
\mat{\sigma}_n\mat{\sigma}_m
=\delta_{nm}\I
+\sum_{k=1}^{3}\epsilon_{nmk}\,i\mat{\sigma}_k.
\label{eq:pauli-matrix-product}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\mat{\sigma}_n^2=\I,
\qquad
\mat{\sigma}_m\mat{\sigma}_n
=-\mat{\sigma}_n\mat{\sigma}_m
\quad(m\neq n),
\qquad
\mat{\sigma}_1\mat{\sigma}_2\mat{\sigma}_3=i\I.
\label{eq:pauli-matrix-basis-products}
\end{equation}
The matrix entry \(i\) is the same central unit defined intrinsically by
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:basis}; no second imaginary unit is introduced.

For \(\pv{Z}=S+\vct{V}\cdot\sigv\), the forward translation is defined by
\begin{align}
\matf{\pv{Z}}
&:=S\I+\sum_{n=1}^{3}V_n\mat{\sigma}_n\notag\\
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
S+V_3&V_1-iV_2\\
V_1+iV_2&S-V_3
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:matrix-paravector-map}
\end{align}
For the generic matrix in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:generic-two-by-two-matrices}, the
inverse translation is defined by
\begin{equation}
\matinvf{\mat{M}}
:=
\frac{m_{11}+m_{22}}{2}
+\frac{m_{12}+m_{21}}{2}\pv{\sigma}_1
+\frac{m_{21}-m_{12}}{2i}\pv{\sigma}_2
+\frac{m_{11}-m_{22}}{2}\pv{\sigma}_3.
\label{eq:matrix-paravector-inverse-map}
\end{equation}
The superscript \(-1\) on the name of the inverse translation belongs to
that function name. It is distinct from the multiplicative matrix inverse
in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:matrix-inverse}. For
\(\pv{Z}\in\CPspace\) and \(\mat{M}\in\C^{2\times2}\), the two descriptions
are related simply by
\begin{equation}
\matf{\pv{Z}}=\mat{M},
\qquad
\pv{Z}=\matinvf{\mat{M}}.
\label{eq:matrix-map-inverses}
\end{equation}
Direct substitution in Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:matrix-paravector-map} and
\eqref{eq:matrix-paravector-inverse-map} verifies both directions.
The following identities are then obtained from the Pauli products in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:pauli-matrix-product}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\matf{\pv{Z}+\pv{Z}'}
&=\matf{\pv{Z}}+\matf{\pv{Z}'},\\
\matf{c\pv{Z}}
&=c\matf{\pv{Z}},
\qquad c\in\C,\\
\matf{\pv{Z}\pv{Z}'}
&=\matf{\pv{Z}}\matf{\pv{Z}'}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-product-preservation}
\end{equation}
Faithfulness of the translation and reproduction of the ordered paravector
product by matrix multiplication are established by these identities.

A fixed matrix is required for the matrix action representing intrinsic
star:
\begin{equation}
\Tmat
:=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\Tmat^{-1}=-\Tmat.
\label{eq:matrix-T}
\end{equation}
For an arbitrary complex matrix, transported star is defined directly by
\begin{equation}
\conj{\mat{M}}
:=\Tmat\,\mconj{\mat{M}}\,\Tmat^{-1}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mconj{m_{22}}&-\mconj{m_{21}}\\
-\mconj{m_{12}}&\mconj{m_{11}}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:matrix-star}
\end{equation}
This transported star is intrinsic star written in matrix coordinates. It
is not entrywise matrix conjugation \(\mathsf C\).

Under the correspondence in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:matrix-map-inverses}, the
complete operation dictionary is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\matf{\conj{\pv{Z}}}
&=\conj{\mat{M}}
=\Tmat\,\mconj{\mat{M}}\,\Tmat^{-1},\\
\matf{\herm{\pv{Z}}}
&=\mherm{\mat{M}}
=\mtrans{\mconj{\mat{M}}},\\
\matf{\adjf{\pv{Z}}}
&=\adjf{\mat{M}}
=\cherm{\mat{M}}
=\Tmat\,\mtrans{\mat{M}}\,\Tmat^{-1},\\
\detf{\pv{Z}}
&=\detf{\mat{M}},\\
\matf{\pv{0}}
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&0\\
0&0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\matf{\pv{1}}=\I,\\
\matf{\pv{Z}^{-1}}
&=\mat{M}^{-1}
=\frac{\adjf{\mat{M}}}{\detf{\mat{M}}},
\qquad \detf{\pv{Z}}\neq0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-operation-dictionary}
\end{equation}
Thus intrinsic \(\Hop\) is represented exactly by the familiar matrix
Hermitian operation, while intrinsic \(*\Hop\) is represented by the matrix
adjugate. In contrast, matrix \(\mathsf C\) is fixed by the chosen complex
entries and has no independent intrinsic-algebra definition. In particular,
\begin{equation}
\conj{\mat{M}}
=\Tmat\,\mconj{\mat{M}}\,\Tmat^{-1}
\neq\mconj{\mat{M}}
\quad\text{in general}.
\label{eq:matrix-star-not-C}
\end{equation}
Under the same correspondence, their distinct effects on the intrinsic
coordinates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\matinvf{\mconj{\mat{M}}}
&=S^*
 +V_1^*\pv{\sigma}_1
 -V_2^*\pv{\sigma}_2
 +V_3^*\pv{\sigma}_3,\\
\matinvf{\mtrans{\mat{M}}}
&=S
 +V_1\pv{\sigma}_1
 -V_2\pv{\sigma}_2
 +V_3\pv{\sigma}_3,\\
\matinvf{\mherm{\mat{M}}}
&=S^*
 +V_1^*\pv{\sigma}_1
 +V_2^*\pv{\sigma}_2
 +V_3^*\pv{\sigma}_3
=\herm{\pv{Z}},\\
\matinvf{\conj{\mat{M}}}
&=S^*
 -V_1^*\pv{\sigma}_1
 -V_2^*\pv{\sigma}_2
 -V_3^*\pv{\sigma}_3
=\conj{\pv{Z}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-coordinate-effects}
\end{equation}
Matrix \(\mathsf C\) and \(\mathsf T\) are basis-dependent: their displayed
coordinate sign patterns are fixed by the particular Pauli matrices in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:pauli-matrices} and can change when different representing
matrices are selected. Their composition \(\mathsf H\), however, represents
the intrinsic Hermitian operation, and transported star represents the
intrinsic star operation.

The extraction functions are translated without additional definitions:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{Z})
&=\frac12\eqfunc{tr}(\mat{M}),\\
\matf{\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{Z})\cdot\sigv}
&=\mat{M}-\frac12\eqfunc{tr}(\mat{M})\,\I,\\
\matf{\eqfunc{real}(\pv{Z})}
&=\frac12\bigl(\mat{M}+\mherm{\mat{M}}\bigr),\\
\matf{\eqfunc{img}(\pv{Z})}
&=\frac{\mat{M}-\mherm{\mat{M}}}{2i}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-extraction-dictionary}
\end{equation}

The product-order distinctions are displayed directly by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mconj{\mat{M}\mat{N}}
&=\mconj{\mat{M}}\mconj{\mat{N}},\\
\mtrans{\mat{M}\mat{N}}
&=\mtrans{\mat{N}}\mtrans{\mat{M}},\\
\mherm{\mat{M}\mat{N}}
&=\mherm{\mat{N}}\mherm{\mat{M}},\\
\conj{\mat{M}\mat{N}}
&=\conj{\mat{M}}\conj{\mat{N}},\\
\adjf{\mat{M}\mat{N}}
&=\adjf{\mat{N}}\adjf{\mat{M}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-operation-products}
\end{equation}
Product order is preserved by matrix \(\mathsf C\) and transported star,
whereas it is reversed by matrix \(\mathsf T\), matrix \(\mathsf H\), and
the adjugate.
The relation
\begin{equation}
\mherm{\mat{M}}
=\conj{\adjf{\mat{M}}}
=\adjf{\conj{\mat{M}}}
\label{eq:matrix-hermitian-adjugate}
\end{equation}
is the matrix translation of
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:hermitian-adjugate-identity}.

The distinction between \(\mathsf C\) and star is made especially visible
by the individual basis matrices:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mconj{\mat{\sigma}_1}&=\mat{\sigma}_1,
&
\mconj{\mat{\sigma}_2}&=-\mat{\sigma}_2,
&
\mconj{\mat{\sigma}_3}&=\mat{\sigma}_3,\\
\mtrans{\mat{\sigma}_1}&=\mat{\sigma}_1,
&
\mtrans{\mat{\sigma}_2}&=-\mat{\sigma}_2,
&
\mtrans{\mat{\sigma}_3}&=\mat{\sigma}_3,\\
\mherm{\mat{\sigma}_n}&=\mat{\sigma}_n,
&
\conj{\mat{\sigma}_n}&=-\mat{\sigma}_n,
&
\adjf{\mat{\sigma}_n}&=-\mat{\sigma}_n.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-basis-actions}
\end{equation}
The same signs happen to be produced on the three individual basis matrices
by entrywise \(\mathsf C\) and transpose, but different operations remain:
product order is preserved by \(\mathsf C\) and reversed by \(\mathsf T\).

For the real event
\(\pv{X}=t+\vct{r}\cdot\sigv\), the translation reduces to
\begin{align}
\matf{\pv{X}}
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
t+r_3&r_1-ir_2\\
r_1+ir_2&t-r_3
\end{bmatrix},
\notag\\
\eqfunc{tr}(\matf{\pv{X}})
&=2t,
\notag\\
\detf{\matf{\pv{X}}}
&=t^2-\vct{r}^{\,2}
=\detf{\pv{X}}.
\label{eq:matrix-X}
\end{align}
Its two eigenvalues are the two roots of
\begin{equation}
x^2-\eqfunc{tr}(\matf{\pv{X}})\,x
+\detf{\matf{\pv{X}}}=0,
\qquad
x=\lambda(\pm)=t\pm\lVert\vct{r}\rVert.
\label{eq:matrix-X-eigenvalues}
\end{equation}
Thus twice the scalar part is recovered by the matrix trace, and the real
space-time quadratic form is recovered by the ordinary matrix determinant.
