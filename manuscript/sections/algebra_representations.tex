\subsubsection{Standard \texorpdfstring{$2\times2$}{2x2} Matrix Realization}
The real associative algebra generated by the three sigma elements is
\(Cl_{3,0}(\R)\). It is isomorphic both to the even space-time algebra
\(Cl^+_{1,3}(\R)\) and to \(2\times2\) complex matrices. The internal
pseudoscalar makes the same algebra a four-dimensional complex vector
space, written in this manuscript as \(\C\oplus\C^3\); this is not the
eight-complex-dimensional algebra \(Cl_3(\C)\). The following faithful
matrix model makes these identifications concrete \cite{pauli1927}.

The standard realization is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_1=\{0, 1; 1, 0\},
\quad
\sigma_2=\{0, -i; i, 0\},
\quad
\sigma_3=\{1, 0; 0, -1\},
\label{eq:pauli-matrices}
\end{equation}
Here the \(i\) appearing in the entries of \(\sigma_2\) is strictly the ordinary complex unit \(i:=\sqrt{-1}\). The matrix entries are not defined recursively from the STA pseudoscalar; rather, the abstract pseudoscalar is represented afterward by the matrix product \(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3=i\I\).
In this realization the basis identities become
\begin{equation*}
\sigma_n^2=\I,
\qquad
\sigma_m\sigma_n=-\sigma_n\sigma_m\quad (m\neq n),
\qquad
\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3=i\I.
\end{equation*}
So Eq.\ \eqref{eq:pauli-matrices} gives a concrete \(2\times2\) model for the generators and their pseudoscalar.

For a real paravector \(X=t+\mathbf r\cdot\sigv\in\R\oplus\R^3\), with \(\mathbf r=(r_1,r_2,r_3)\in\R^3\), the matrix form is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
X&=t+\mathbf r\cdot\sigv
=\{ t+r_3, r_1-ir_2; r_1+ir_2, t-r_3 \},\\
\det(X)&=t^2-\mathbf r^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:matrix-X}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:matrix-X} reproduces exactly the real Minkowski quadratic form already encoded by Eq.\ \eqref{eq:real-paravector-metric}. The same matrix also carries the familiar trace and characteristic data:
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{tr}(X)&=2t,\\
\lambda^2-\operatorname{tr}(X)\lambda+\det(X)&=0,\\
\lambda(\pm)&=t\pm \lVert\mathbf r\rVert.
\end{align*}
These eigenvalues are the matrix version of the split into scalar part \(t\) and vector magnitude \(\lVert\mathbf r\rVert\).

More generally, \(Z=S+\mathbf V\cdot\sigv\) represents an arbitrary
\(2\times2\) complex matrix, and
\begin{align*}
\eqtext{scalar}(Z)&=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(Z),&
\det(Z)&=S^2-\mathbf V^2,\\
Z^{*\mathsf R}&=\operatorname{adj}(Z).
\end{align*}
Consequently, the manuscript's determinant quadratic form is the ordinary
matrix determinant in this realization:
\begin{equation*}
ZZ^{*\mathsf R}=\det(Z)\I.
\end{equation*}

Within the same realization, the matrix involutions can also be written explicitly. Let \((\cdot)^c\) denote entrywise complex conjugation and let \((\cdot)^{cT}\) denote complex conjugate transpose. Then
\begin{equation*}
i^c=-i,
\qquad
\sigma_1^c=\sigma_1,
\qquad
\sigma_2^c=-\sigma_2,
\qquad
\sigma_3^c=\sigma_3,
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
(\sigma_n)^{cT}&=\sigma_n,\\
(\sigma_n\sigma_m)^{cT}&=\sigma_m\sigma_n=-\sigma_n\sigma_m
\qquad (n\neq m),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
(i\I)^{cT}&=-i\I,\\
(i\sigma_n)^{cT}
&=\bigl((\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3)\sigma_n\bigr)^{cT}\\
&=\sigma_n^{cT}(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3)^{cT}\\
&=-i\sigma_n,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
and therefore
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\I^{\mathrm R}&=\I,
&
\sigma_n^{\mathrm R}&=\sigma_n,\\
(i\I)^{\mathrm R}&=-i\I,
&
(i\sigma_n)^{\mathrm R}&=-i\sigma_n,\\
(\cdot)^{\mathrm R}&=(\cdot)^{cT}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
So in the matrix model the reverse operator coincides with the Hermitian adjoint. The sigma conjugation formulas are generated by the fixed matrix
\begin{equation*}
\Mmat:=\{0, 1; -1, 0\},
\qquad
\Mmat^{-1}=-\Mmat.
\end{equation*}
With this matrix one finds
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\I^{*}&=\I,
&
\sigma_n^{*}&=-\sigma_n,\\
(i\I)^{*}&=-i\I,
&
(i\sigma_n)^{*}&=i\sigma_n,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
(\cdot)^{*}&=\Mmat\,(\cdot)^{c}\,\Mmat^{-1},\\
(\cdot)^{*\mathrm R}=(\cdot)^{\mathrm R*}
&=\Mmat\,(\cdot)^{T}\,\Mmat^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus the matrix section now contains, in one place, the basis matrices, the spacetime paravector matrix, the determinant/trace/eigenvalue formulas, complex conjugation, complex-conjugate transpose, reverse, sigma conjugation, and reverse sigma conjugation. These concrete formulas match the abstract operator rules defined earlier in the algebraic development.

The operator types are worth making explicit. Both \((\cdot)^*\) and
\((\cdot)^{\mathsf R}\) are real-linear but complex-antilinear because each
sends the central pseudoscalar \(i\) to \(-i\). The first is an
automorphism, whereas the second is an anti-automorphism. Their composition
\((\cdot)^{*\mathsf R}\) is a complex-linear anti-automorphism. In the
matrix model, reversion is Hermitian adjunction and reverse conjugation is
the adjugate map displayed above.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary Block Map}
It is also useful to package a scalar-coefficient complex paravector
\(Z\in\C\oplus\C^3\) into the \(2\times2\) block map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-map}
\Wmat(Z):=\{0, Z; \crev{Z}, 0\}.
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Wmat(Z)^2=\mathrm{diag}(Z\crev{Z},\crev{Z}Z)
=\langle\!\langle Z\rangle\!\rangle^2\I.
\label{eq:W-square}
\end{equation}
Here \(\Wmat(Z)\) is a \(2\times2\) matrix over the paravector algebra. After
each block is replaced by its Pauli matrix, it is a \(4\times4\) complex
matrix. It is a useful linear packaging map, not an algebra homomorphism.
Equation \eqref{eq:W-square} follows because its diagonal block products
reduce to the determinant quadratic form in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:minkowski-form};
the off-diagonal blocks vanish \cite{hestenes1966,doran2003}.

If \(\langle\!\langle Z\rangle\!\rangle^2\neq0\), the annihilating
polynomial \(x^2-\langle\!\langle Z\rangle\!\rangle^2\) has distinct roots,
so the block matrix is diagonalizable after a square-root branch is chosen.
If
\(\langle\!\langle Z\rangle\!\rangle^2=0\) and \(Z\neq0\), then
\(\Wmat(Z)^2=0\) while \(\Wmat(Z)\neq0\): it is nilpotent and not
diagonalizable. For differential or other noncommuting operator-valued
blocks, only the first ordered diagonal identity in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:W-square} is automatic; reduction to one scalar multiple of
the identity requires a separate operator calculation.
