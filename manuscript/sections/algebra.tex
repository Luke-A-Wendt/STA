\subsubsection*{Sigma Algebra}
The sigma generators and their multiplication rules are written out here explicitly so that every later paravector identity can be traced back to the defining algebra. See Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:basis} and \eqref{eq:sigma-product}. For \(n,m\in\{1,2,3\}\),
\begin{equation*}
\sigma _{n}^{2} = +1, \sigma _{n}\sigma _{m} = -\sigma _{m}\sigma _{n} \text{ if } m \neq n.
\end{equation*}
The first useful consequence is the pseudoscalar relation \(i=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\). This fixes the sign convention used later.
\noindent Using Eq.\ \eqref{eq:sigma-product},
\begin{align*}
\sigma _{1}\sigma _{2}\sigma _{3} &= (\delta _{12} + \epsilon _{123} i \sigma _{3}) \sigma _{3},\notag \\
&= (0 + i \sigma _{3}) \sigma _{3} = i.
\end{align*}
\subsubsection*{Conjugate-Operator Action on Sigma Products}
The conjugate-operator identities derived from Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:operator-rules}--\eqref{eq:i-operator-signs} take the following explicit sigma-product form.\par
\begin{align*}
\sigma _{n}^{*} &= -\sigma _{n},\notag \\
i^{*} &= -i,\notag \\
(i \sigma _{n} )^{*} &= i \sigma _{n},\notag \\
(\sigma _{1}\sigma _{2}\sigma _{3})^{*} &= \sigma _{1}^{*} \sigma _{2}^{*} \sigma _{3}^{*}\qquad \text{does not flip order},\notag \\
&= (-\sigma _{1})(-\sigma _{2})(-\sigma _{3}),\notag \\
&= -\sigma _{1}\sigma _{2}\sigma _{3}.
\end{align*}
The point is that the conjugate operator changes the sign of each sigma-number-one factor without reversing multiplication order. The pseudoscalar is therefore odd under the conjugate operator, whereas the sigma-number-two basis elements remain even.
\begin{align*}
( i \sigma _{n} )^{*} &= i^{*} \sigma _{n}^{*},\notag \\
&= (-i)(-\sigma _{n}),\notag \\
&= i \sigma _{n}.
\end{align*}

\subsubsection*{Reverse-Operator Action on Sigma Products}
The reverse-operator identities derived from Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:operator-rules}--\eqref{eq:i-operator-signs} take the following explicit sigma-product form.\par
\begin{align*}
\sigma _{n}^{\mathrm{R}} &= \sigma _{n},\notag \\
(\sigma _{1}\sigma _{2}\sigma _{3})^{\mathrm{R}} &= \sigma _{3}\sigma _{2}\sigma _{1},\notag \\
&= -\sigma _{1}\sigma _{2}\sigma _{3},\notag \\
i^{\mathrm{R}} &= -i.
\end{align*}
Thus the reverse leaves each sigma-number-one generator fixed but changes the sign of the pseudoscalar by reversing the multiplication order.
\begin{align*}
(i \sigma _{n})^{\mathrm{R}} &= -i \sigma _{n},\notag \\
(i \sigma _{n})^{\mathrm{R}} &= \sigma _{n} ( i )^{\mathrm{R}},\notag \\
&= \sigma _{n} (-i),\notag \\
&= -i \sigma _{n}.
\end{align*}

\subsubsection*{Complex Paravector-Valued Maps}
This notation records how real scalar and vector functions combine into a generic complex paravector-valued map without imposing any physical interpretation.
\begin{align*}
Z(t,\mathbf r) &= \bigl(a(t,\mathbf r)+i\,b(t,\mathbf r)\bigr)\notag\\
&\quad + \bigl(\mathbf A(t,\mathbf r)+i\,\mathbf B(t,\mathbf r)\bigr)\cdot\sigv,\\
a,b &: \R\times\R^3 \Rightarrow \R,\\
\mathbf A,\mathbf B &: \R\times\R^3 \Rightarrow \R^3,\\
Z &: \R\times\R^3 \Rightarrow \C\oplus\C^3.
\end{align*}
