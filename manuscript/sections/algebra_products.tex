\subsubsection{Order functions}
For arbitrary algebra elements \(\pv{A}\) and \(\pv{B}\), the commutator
function is defined by
\begin{equation}
\com{\pv{A}}{\pv{B}}:=\pv{A}\pv{B}-\pv{B}\pv{A}.
\label{eq:com-function}
\end{equation}
The anticommutator function is defined independently by
\begin{equation}
\acom{\pv{A}}{\pv{B}}:=\pv{A}\pv{B}+\pv{B}\pv{A}.
\label{eq:acom-function}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Products of real scalars, vectors, and paravectors}
For the real paravectors \(\pv{X}\) and \(\pv{Y}\) from
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:real-paravector-x} and \eqref{eq:real-paravector-y},
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dot-cross} gives
\begin{align*}
\pv{X}\pv{Y}
&= (a+\vct{A}\cdot\sigv)(b+\vct{B}\cdot\sigv)\notag\\
&= (ab+\vct{A}\cdot\vct{B})
+ \bigl(a\vct{B}+b\vct{A}+i(\vct{A}\times\vct{B})\bigr)\cdot\sigv,\\
\pv{Y}\pv{X}
&= (ab+\vct{A}\cdot\vct{B})
+ \bigl(a\vct{B}+b\vct{A}-i(\vct{A}\times\vct{B})\bigr)\cdot\sigv \notag\\
&= (\pv{X}\pv{Y})^{\mathsf H}.
\end{align*}
The same product is given in component form in
Table \ref{tab:components-real-paravector-product}. The scalar part is
symmetric, while the sign of the imaginary-vector part is changed by the
order.

Two immediate consequences are
\begin{align*}
\com{\pv{X}}{\pv{Y}} &= 2i(\vct{A}\times\vct{B})\cdot\sigv,\\
\pv{X}\pv{Y}\in\R+\R^3
&\quad\eqtext{if and only if}\quad \vct{A}\times\vct{B}=0.
\end{align*}
Thus exact commutation of two real paravectors is obtained precisely when
their vector parts are parallel.

The antisymmetric term is removed automatically by setting
\(\pv{Y}=\pv{X}\):
\begin{align*}
\pv{X}^2
&= a^2+\vct{A}^2+2a\,\vct{A}\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
If \(\pv{X}^2=b+\vct{B}\cdot\sigv\), the following relations are obtained by
matching scalar and vector parts:
\begin{align*}
b &= a^2+\vct{A}^2,\\
\vct{B} &= 2a\,\vct{A},
\end{align*}
hence
\begin{align*}
a^2 &= \frac{1}{2}\Bigl(b\pm\sqrt{b^2-\vct{B}^2}\Bigr),\\
\vct{A}^2 &= b-a^2.
\end{align*}
These equations are necessary, but the direction equation
\(\vct{B}=2a\vct{A}\) must also be retained. When
\(\vct{B}\neq\vct{0}\), real paravector roots exist precisely when
\(b\geq\lVert\vct{B}\rVert\). For either choice of the inner sign, either
sign of the resulting nonzero \(a\) may be chosen, with
\begin{equation*}
\vct{A}=\frac{\vct{B}}{2a}.
\end{equation*}
When \(\vct{B}=\vct{0}\), a real root requires \(b\geq0\); the scalar
roots are \(\pv{X}=\pm\sqrt{b}\), and the degenerate pure-vector roots satisfy
\(a=0\) and \(\vct{A}^2=b\). Thus the displayed quadratic relation is a
branch condition, rather than by itself a complete square-root formula.

\subsubsection{Products of complex paravectors}
The master product law \eqref{eq:paravector-product} is the complex extension of the real formulas. When \(\pv{Z}\) is written as in Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:paravector}--\eqref{eq:paravector-cartesian} and \(\pv{Z}'=\pv{X}'+i\pv{Y}'=S'+\vct{V}'\cdot\sigv\), every specialization is obtained by setting the unused scalar or vector parts to zero. The quantities \(S,S'\) and the components of \(\vct{V},\vct{V}'\) are central complex scalars. Scalar coefficients may therefore be passed through one another and through the sigma generators. The full component decomposition of \(\pv{Z}\pv{Z}'\) is given in Table \ref{tab:components-paravector-product}, that of \(\pv{Z}^2\) is given in Table \ref{tab:components-paravector-square}, and the order-sensitive products built from the conjugate and Hermitian operations are recorded in Tables \ref{tab:components-zzstar}--\ref{tab:components-zcrevz}.

Within this finite-dimensional scalar-coefficient algebra, the scalar part is cyclic:
\begin{equation*}
\eqtext{scalar}(\pv{Z}\pv{Z}')=\eqtext{scalar}(\pv{Z}'\pv{Z}).
\end{equation*}
In particular, if \(\pv{Z}'=\pv{M}\pv{Z}^{*\mathsf H}\), then
\begin{align*}
\eqtext{scalar}(\pv{Z}\pv{M}\pv{Z}^{*\mathsf H})
&=\eqtext{scalar}(\pv{M}\pv{Z}^{*\mathsf H}\pv{Z})\notag\\
&=\eqtext{scalar}(\pv{M})\,(\pv{Z}^{*\mathsf H}\pv{Z}),
\end{align*}
because \(\pv{Z}^{*\mathsf H}\pv{Z}\) is scalar.

The order-sensitive variants are divided into three algebraic types:
\begin{align*}
\pv{Z}\pv{Z}^* &\eqtext{ is a real scalar plus a complex vector},\\
\pv{Z}\pv{Z}^{\mathsf H} &\eqtext{ is a real scalar plus a real vector},\\
\pv{Z}\pv{Z}^{*\mathsf H} &\eqtext{ is a complex scalar}.
\end{align*}
The positive norms, determinant forms, inverses, and transformation formulas
are supplied by those three cases.

If a component is instead a differential or another noncommuting operator,
the ordered expansion must be used before any simplification: in general
\(\vct{V} S'\neq S'\vct{V}\), Leibniz terms may occur, and cyclicity of
the algebraic scalar projection alone does not justify cycling the operator
coefficients. The displayed ordering is therefore required in operator
applications, which cannot be obtained by automatic substitution into the
central-coefficient formulas.

\subsubsection{Order dependence and commutators}
The order-dependent part of a product is represented by the commutator
function defined in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:com-function}. For generic complex paravectors
\(\pv{Z},\pv{Z}'\in\C+\C^3\),
\begin{equation}
\com{\pv{Z}}{\pv{Z}'}
= 2i(\vct{V}\times\vct{V}')\cdot\sigv
\label{eq:paravector-commutator}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:paravector-commutator} is the compact commutator law. In
real and imaginary vector components, the following expansion is obtained:
\begin{align*}
\com{\pv{Z}}{\pv{Z}'}
&= -2(\vct{A}\times\vct{B}'+\vct{B}\times\vct{A}')\cdot\sigv \notag\\
&\quad +2i(\vct{A}\times\vct{A}'-\vct{B}\times\vct{B}')\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
The real-paravector commutator
\begin{equation*}
\com{\pv{X}}{\pv{Y}}=2i(\vct{A}\times\vct{B})\cdot\sigv
\end{equation*}
is the corresponding specialization. In every case the scalar terms are canceled,
so the cross-product part of the multiplication law is isolated by the
commutator.
The following identity is obtained by contracting the basis commutators
with a second vector:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n=1}^3 B_n\com{\vct{A}\cdot\sigv}{\pv{\sigma}_n}
=\com{\vct{A}\cdot\sigv}{\vct{B}\cdot\sigv}
=2i(\vct{A}\times\vct{B})\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:sigma-commutator-contraction}
\end{equation}
This is the component bridge between the vector cross-product law and the
commutator correction in the paravector Leibniz rule.

The symmetric companion is the anticommutator from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:acom-function}. For pure vectors,
\begin{align}
\acom{\vct{A}\cdot\sigv}{\vct{B}\cdot\sigv}
&=(\vct{A}\cdot\sigv)(\vct{B}\cdot\sigv)\notag\\
&\quad+(\vct{B}\cdot\sigv)(\vct{A}\cdot\sigv)\notag\\
&=2\,\vct{A}\cdot\vct{B}.
\label{eq:vector-anticommutator}
\end{align}
Thus the symmetric dot-product part is isolated by
\(\acom{\mathord\cdot}{\mathord\cdot}\), just as the antisymmetric
cross-product part is isolated by \(\com{\mathord\cdot}{\mathord\cdot}\).

More generally, for generic complex paravectors
\(\pv{Z},\pv{Z}'\in\C+\C^3\), Eq.\ \eqref{eq:acom-function} gives
\begin{equation}
\acom{\pv{Z}}{\pv{Z}'}=\pv{Z}\pv{Z}'+\pv{Z}'\pv{Z}.
\label{eq:paravector-anticommutator-expansion}
\end{equation}
The anticommutator is therefore
\begin{equation}
\acom{\pv{Z}}{\pv{Z}'}
=2\bigl(S S'+\vct{V}\cdot\vct{V}'\bigr)
+2\bigl(S\vct{V}'+S'\vct{V}\bigr)\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:paravector-anticommutator}
\end{equation}
The cross-product contribution to the anticommutator is canceled identically.
Only the symmetric scalar and vector couplings are retained.

The same order dependence is already present in the sigma basis itself.
Commutation of the pseudoscalar with the generators is expressed by
\begin{align*}
\com{i}{\pv{\sigma}_n} &= 0,\\
i\sigv &= \sigv i = \{\pv{\sigma}_2\pv{\sigma}_3,\ \pv{\sigma}_3\pv{\sigma}_1,\ \pv{\sigma}_1\pv{\sigma}_2\},
\end{align*}
while the following relation is satisfied by distinct sigma generators:
\begin{equation*}
\com{\pv{\sigma}_n}{\pv{\sigma}_m}=2\pv{\sigma}_n\pv{\sigma}_m=2\sum_{k=1}^3\epsilon_{nmk}i\pv{\sigma}_k
\qquad (n\neq m).
\end{equation*}
Thus the oriented sigma-number-two basis is exactly the commutator closure of the sigma-number-one generators.
