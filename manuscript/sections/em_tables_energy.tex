energy-density/Poynting paravector
\(\pv{T}=\tfrac12 \pv{F} \pv{F}^{\mathsf H}\), whose components depend on
the chosen inertial frame, is given in Table \ref{tab:components-ffrev}.
\begin{componenttable}[tab:components-fsquare]{\tfrac12\pv{F}^{2}}
\componentrow{real scalar}{\tfrac12(\vct{E}^{2}-\vct{B}^{2})}{}
\componentrow{img scalar}{\vct{E}\cdot\vct{B}}{}
\componentrow{real vector}{\vct{0}}{}
\componentrow{img vector}{\vct{0}}{}
\end{componenttable}

\begin{componenttable}[tab:components-ffrev]{\tfrac12\pv{F} \pv{F}^{\mathsf H}}
\componentrow{scalar}{\tfrac12(\vct{E}^{2}+\vct{B}^{2})}{energy density}
\componentrow{vector}{\vct{E}\times\vct{B}}{Poynting vector}
\end{componenttable}

\noindent
The differentiated field products and the products containing the source
are displayed in the next five tables. They are algebraic identities and
consequences of Maxwell's equation
\(\pv{\partial}\pv{F}=\pv{D}^{*}\), not additional field equations.
The algebra products do not generally commute:
\(\pv{A}\pv{B}\) need not equal \(\pv{B}\pv{A}\). The spatial part of the
derivative acts from the left, so differentiating a product requires the
ordered correction in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:paravector-leibniz}. For the
energy-density/Poynting paravector, the result is
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\partial}\pv{T}
=\frac12(\pv{\partial}\pv{F})\pv{F}^{\mathsf H}
+\frac12\pv{F}(\pv{\partial}\pv{F}^{\mathsf H})
+\frac12\sum_{n=1}^{3}
\com{\pv{\sigma}_n}{\pv{F}}\,\partial_n\pv{F}^{\mathsf H}.
\end{equation*}
The component split of the left side is therefore the derived identity
shown next.
\begin{componenttable}[tab:components-partial-t]{\pv{\partial} \pv{T}}
\componentrow{real scalar}{\partial_t\eqtext{scalar}(\pv{T})
+\gradv\cdot\eqtext{vector}(\pv{T})}{}
\componentrow{img scalar}{0}{}
\componentrow{real vector}{\gradv\eqtext{scalar}(\pv{T})
+\partial_t\eqtext{vector}(\pv{T})}{}
\componentrow{img vector}{\gradv\times\eqtext{vector}(\pv{T})}{}
\end{componenttable}

Because \(\pv{F}^{2}\) is a complex scalar, its derivative can also be
expanded directly:
\begin{componenttable}[tab:components-partial-fsquare]{\pv{\partial}(\pv{F}^{2})}
\componentrow{real scalar}{\partial_t(\vct{E}^{2}-\vct{B}^{2})}{}
\componentrow{img scalar}{\partial_t\!\big(2\,\vct{E}\cdot\vct{B}\big)}{}
\componentrow{real vector}{\gradv(\vct{E}^{2}-\vct{B}^{2})}{}
\componentrow{img vector}{\gradv\!\big(2\,\vct{E}\cdot\vct{B}\big)}{}
\end{componenttable}

The two ordered source products are given by Maxwell's equation:
\begin{componenttable}[tab:components-fdstar]{\pv{F} \pv{D}^{*}}
\componentrow{real scalar}{-\vct{E}\cdot\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{img scalar}{-\vct{B}\cdot\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{real vector}{\rho\vct{E}+\vct{B}\times\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{img vector}{\rho\vct{B}-\vct{E}\times\vct{J}}{}
\end{componenttable}

\begin{componenttable}[tab:components-dstarf]{\pv{D}^{*}\pv{F}}
\componentrow{real scalar}{-\vct{E}\cdot\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{img scalar}{-\vct{B}\cdot\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{real vector}{\rho\vct{E}-\vct{B}\times\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{img vector}{\rho\vct{B}+\vct{E}\times\vct{J}}{}
\end{componenttable}

Their sum in the two possible product orders is
\begin{componenttable}[tab:components-dstarf-plus-fdstar]{\pv{D}^{*}\pv{F}+\pv{F} \pv{D}^{*}}
\componentrow{real scalar}{-2\,\vct{E}\cdot\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{img scalar}{-2\,\vct{B}\cdot\vct{J}}{}
\componentrow{real vector}{2\rho\vct{E}}{}
\componentrow{img vector}{2\rho\vct{B}}{}
\end{componenttable}

\noindent
Only part of the derivative of \(\pv{F}^{2}\) is contained in the last table. The complete
identity is
\begin{align*}
\pv{\partial}(\pv{F}^{2})
&=\pv{D}^{*}\pv{F}+\pv{F}\pv{D}^{*}
+\sum_{n=1}^{3}\com{\pv{\sigma}_n}{\pv{F}}\,\partial_n\pv{F}.
\end{align*}
With \(\pv{F}=\vct{F}\cdot\sigv\), the correction term is
\begin{align*}
\sum_{n=1}^{3}\com{\pv{\sigma}_n}{\pv{F}}\,\partial_n\pv{F}
&=-2i\,\vct{F}\cdot(\gradv\times\vct{F})\notag\\
&\quad
-2\left((\gradv\cdot\vct{F})\vct{F}
-\frac12\gradv(\vct{F}^{2})\right)\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
It is precisely the correction that would be lost by treating the ordered
paravector factors as though they commuted while applying the ordinary
two-term derivative product rule.
