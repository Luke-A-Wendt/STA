\subsubsection{Relativistic Quantum-Mechanical Setting}
One fixed operator language is used throughout the wave-equation section. With the differential conventions from Section \ref{sec:differential},
\begin{align*}
\pv{\partial}&=\partial_t+\sigv\cdot\gradv,\\
\conj{\pv{\partial}}&=\partial_t-\sigv\cdot\gradv,\\
\conj{(i\pv{\partial})}&=-i\conj{\pv{\partial}}.
\end{align*}
The four-dimensional wave operator, conventionally called the
d'Alembertian \cite{greiner2000}, is
\begin{align*}
\Box
&=\partial_t^2-\gradv^2\notag\\
&=\pv{\partial}\conj{\pv{\partial}}
=\conj{\pv{\partial}}\pv{\partial}.
\end{align*}
All fields are acted on by the differential operators to their left. In an ordered
product, the action of the rightmost factor is applied first, and the written order is retained
whenever potentials and derivatives do not commute.

\subsubsection{Algebra Blocks and Fixed-Projector Wave Functions}
Throughout the spinful part of the wave-equation section, a wave field is not a
space-time event but an algebra-valued amplitude field.  A generic algebra
block may be written with the scalar and vector extraction functions from
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:scalar-function} and \eqref{eq:vector-function}:
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}&:\R\times\R^3\to\CPspace,\\
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}(t,\vct{r})
&=\eqfunc{scalar}\!\bigl(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}(t,\vct{r})\bigr)\notag\\
&\quad+\eqfunc{vector}\!\bigl(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}(t,\vct{r})\bigr)\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
The location at which the state is evaluated in space-time is specified by the argument \((t,\vct{r})\), while the value \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}(t,\vct{r})\) is contained in the internal complex-paravector algebra. The real paravector
\begin{equation*}
\pv{X}=t+\vct{r}\cdot\sigv
\end{equation*}
is used to describe an event or interval variable, whereas \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\) is a quantum state amplitude defined \emph{over} such events.

At each fixed point, four complex coefficients are contained in an
unrestricted complex paravector. A single two-amplitude block is selected
without adding component indices by the fixed right projector
\(\pv{\Pi}(+)\) from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:fixed-column-carrier}. A physical block is restricted by
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}=\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\pv{\Pi}(+).
\end{equation*}
Using the ladder element from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:ladder}, every such block has
the unique algebraic expansion given in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:fixed-projector-amplitudes}.
Every displayed operator is applied on the left, so the restriction is
preserved by associativity:
\((\pv{Z}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\pv{\Pi}(+)
=\pv{Z}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\pv{\Pi}(+))=\pv{Z}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\).
The fixed right factor is only a carrier selector; it is not a measurement
of spin along the third axis.

The scalar and vector coefficients of an algebra block are amplitude channels,
not separately measurable particles or classical subfields.  A bare component
count also does not cause physical spin to be assigned: the transformation law, field
equation, and fixed-projector restriction must be specified together.

The field \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\in\C\) is used in the spin-0 Klein--Gordon subsections. A
two-block field is used in the Dirac construction and is treated as the
ordered pair
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}=(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}),
\end{equation*}
with \(n\in\{1,2\}\) and
\(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},n}=\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},n}\pv{\Pi}(+)\). Two complex amplitudes are
then carried by each block, and the four complex amplitudes of one Dirac
field are carried by the pair. If the fixed-projector restriction is
omitted, two independent Dirac fields are obtained by decomposition with
the complementary right projectors, and the same equations remain
algebraically valid for both.

\subsubsection{Complex-Paravector State Pairings}
In the conventional bra-ket terminology, the state on the right of a
pairing is called a ket, while its Hermitian counterpart on the left is
called a bra \cite{greiner2000,sakurai2017}. That terminology is made
precise in the paravector setting by direct use of the already defined
scalar and vector extraction functions:
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
&=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
 +\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\cdot\sigv,\\
\pv{Z}
&=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{Z})
 +\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{Z})\cdot\sigv,\\
\pv{X}
&=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{X})
 +\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{X})\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
Here \(\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}),\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{Z})\in\C\),
\(\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}),\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{Z})\in\C^3\),
\(\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{X})\in\R\), and
\(\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{X})\in\R^3\). Every differential operator acts on
the field to its right. The three involutions on the state
are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\conj{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}&=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*-\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*\cdot\sigv,\\
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}&=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*\cdot\sigv,\\
\cherm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}&=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})-\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\cdot\sigv.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The positive pairing follows directly from the fixed-projector expansion.
For complex coefficients \(a,b,c,d\), the two fields are written as
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}=a\pv{\Pi}(+)+b\pv{a}(-),
\qquad
\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}=c\pv{\Pi}(+)+d\pv{a}(-).
\end{equation*}
From Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:projectors}, \eqref{eq:ladder}, and
\eqref{eq:ladder-properties}, the following identities are obtained:
\(\herm{\pv{\Pi}(+)}=\pv{\Pi}(+)\),
\(\herm{\pv{a}(-)}=\pv{a}(+)\),
\(\pv{\Pi}(+)\pv{a}(-)=0\),
\(\pv{a}(+)\pv{\Pi}(+)=0\), and
\(\pv{a}(+)\pv{a}(-)=\pv{\Pi}(+)\). Consequently, the product
reduces within the algebra as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\\
&\quad=\bigl(c^*\pv{\Pi}(+)+d^*\pv{a}(+)\bigr)
  \bigl(a\pv{\Pi}(+)+b\pv{a}(-)\bigr)\\
&\quad=(c^*a+d^*b)\pv{\Pi}(+),\\
&2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\left(
\herm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
\right)\\
&\quad=c^*a+d^*b.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hermitian-column-pairing}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\herm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\) supplies the bra associated with the selected ket,
and
\(2\,\eqfunc{scalar}(\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\) is the sum of the
absolute squares of the two amplitudes. This nonnegative sum is the squared
norm of the projected block. The factor \(2\) is required because
\(\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\Pi}(+))=1/2\). By summation of this
relation over the two projected Dirac blocks, the density is obtained; the
spatial current is instead obtained from the opposite-order block products
fixed by the Dirac equation.
The basic component products are collected below.

For any real scalar multiplier \(S\), the three-factor Hermitian product is
\begin{equation*}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}S\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
=S\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}.
\end{equation*}
The case \(S=1\) gives the physical fixed-projector density defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projected-block-density-function}. Its local form is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
&=2\,\eqfunc{scalar}(\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\
&=2|\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})|^2\\
&\quad+2\,\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*
 \cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\
&=|a|^2+|b|^2\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The amplitudes from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:fixed-projector-amplitudes} are used in
the last line. The factor is retained
explicitly in the scalar-extraction formula.
The squared-norm density on one fixed-time spatial slice for a projected Pauli
state and, after summation over both blocks, for a Dirac state is supplied
by this positive scalar. It is not the
conserved Klein--Gordon norm of a scalar relativistic field. The vector part of
\(\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\), however, is not automatically a spatial probability
current. In fact, under the convention
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}'=\herm{\pv{R}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\), in which the state is rotated while
the basis is held fixed, it is unchanged:
\begin{equation*}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}'}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}'
=\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{R}\herm{\pv{R}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
=\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}.
\end{equation*}
Spatial spin information instead occurs in products with the
opposite order, such as \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\), or in three-factor
operator products fixed by
the field equation. If \(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\) acts from the left and preserves
the fixed-projector condition, then
\begin{equation*}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
=2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\right)
\pv{\Pi}(+).
\end{equation*}
Thus only one complex pairing coefficient is independent in this ordered
product; its fixed vector part records the selected fixed-projector space. Four
complex coefficient channels can occur only when the fixed-projector
restriction is removed or a different product order is used.
No generic physical interpretation is assigned to such unrestricted
nonscalar channels.

For a nonzero projected block, the scale is defined by
\begin{equation}
N:=\rho(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}).
\label{eq:projected-block-scale}
\end{equation}
The following useful opposite-order identities are obtained from the
fixed-projector condition:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}&=N\pv{\Pi}(+),\\
\herm{\left(\frac{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}}{N}\right)}
&=\frac{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}}{N},\\
\left(\frac{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}}{N}\right)^2
&=\frac{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:projected-block-density}
\end{equation}
Only the selected fixed-projector space is retained by the first product. The
Hermitian idempotent in the second and third lines represents one fully
specified two-amplitude state \cite{sakurai2017}. The conventional name
``pure state'' means that one definite state, rather than a statistical
mixture of several possible states, is represented. Its determinant is zero,
and twice the vector part of
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}/N\) gives the three normalized spin-axis
imbalances of that state. Without division by \(N\), the same vector is the
norm-weighted local spin density. This is the familiar geometry of a
two-amplitude state, not an additional paravector degree of freedom.

At fixed time, the spatially integrated bracket defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:adjoint-paravector-braket} is
\begin{equation*}
\langle \pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}} \mid \pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}} \rangle
=2\iiint\eqfunc{scalar}(\herm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\,
\dd r_1\,\dd r_2\,\dd r_3.
\end{equation*}
By Eq.\ \eqref{eq:hermitian-column-pairing}, scalar extraction is converted
into the ordinary inner product of the two projected amplitudes by the
factor \(2\). It is therefore a normalization convention rather than an
additional physical factor.
For a projected one-block Pauli state, normalization means that the
equal-field specialization is one:
\begin{equation*}
\langle \pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\mid\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\rangle
=\iiint\rho(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\,\dd r_1\,\dd r_2\,\dd r_3
=1.
\end{equation*}
For a complex-paravector operator \(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\),
\begin{equation*}
\langle \pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\mid\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\rangle
=2\iiint\eqfunc{scalar}(\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\,
\dd r_1\,\dd r_2\,\dd r_3.
\end{equation*}
For a state that is not already normalized, the scalar pairing produced when
the operator acts between the bra and ket is divided by the squared length:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\langle\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\mid\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\rangle}
{\langle\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\mid\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\rangle}.
\label{eq:expectation-map}
\end{equation}
Suppose the operator output is
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
=\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{operator\_output}}.
\end{equation*}
Its scalar--vector decomposition is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{operator\_output}}\\
&\qquad=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{operator\_output}})\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad
+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{operator\_output}})\cdot\sigv.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The local scalar integrand is then
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\eqfunc{scalar}\bigl(
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*\,
 \eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{operator\_output}})
\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*
 \cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{operator\_output}}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
After spatial integration and normalization by
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:expectation-map}, the result is real and is called the
expectation value when \(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\) is Hermitian under that integral
and every boundary contribution vanishes. It is the average result associated
with repeated measurements of that operator in the specified state
\cite{sakurai2017}. The local integrand need not itself be real. For a
general \(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\), the normalized result is only a pairing
coefficient and may be complex.
For the Dirac field the physical norm is the sum of this same fixed-projector
pairing over its two restricted blocks; the associated conserved spatial
current is supplied by the field equation. No separately conserved norm is
assigned to either massive Dirac block.

By contrast, for an unrestricted algebra-valued paravector field,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\adjf{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\\
&\qquad=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})^2\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad-\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
\cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\
&\qquad=\detf{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
is the determinant quadratic form. It is algebraically useful, but it is not
positive-definite: a positive-definite form must be real and strictly
positive for every nonzero input, whereas this determinant can be complex,
can take either sign when it is real, and can vanish for a nonzero input. It
should therefore not be used as the probabilistic normalization. Moreover,
after the fixed-projector restriction, this determinant vanishes identically
as follows directly from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:fixed-projector-amplitudes} and the projector--ladder
products. In this framework the single bra-ket bracket
\(\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle\) is therefore reserved for the scalar
pairing built from \(\Hop\).

\subsubsection{Wave-Function Spaces and Pairings}
In standard physics, spin is not assigned from component count alone. The physically relevant data are
\begin{equation*}
(\text{transformation law},\ \text{field equation},\ \text{constraints}).
\end{equation*}
Thus the spin-zero Klein--Gordon field is carried by a complex scalar only
after its scalar transformation law and field equation are imposed. A
one-half-unit Dirac block is carried by a projected two-amplitude block only after
the paired first-order equations are imposed. The spin-one electromagnetic
field is carried by the pure-vector complex paravector \(\pv{F}\) only with
its field transformation law and Maxwell equation. A connection between the present
construction and the standard classification is made by these statements in
words, without introduction of another symbolic notation.

\noindent\textbf{Scalar pairings.}
For two complex-paravector fields \(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}\) and \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\), the three scalar pairings are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\eqfunc{scalar}\bigl(
\herm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*\,
\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*
\cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}),\\
&\eqfunc{scalar}\bigl(
\cherm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}})\,
\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad-\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}})
\cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}),\\
&\eqfunc{scalar}\bigl(
\conj{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*\,
\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad-\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}})^*
\cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The first and third lines are conjugate-linear in \(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}\) and linear
in \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\): multiplication of \(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}\) by a complex number
conjugates that number in the result, whereas multiplication of
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\) does not. The second line is linear in both fields. When the
two fields agree, the first line is the positive Hermitian pairing
\cite{greiner2000,sakurai2017};
the second is the determinant extension; and the third is its indefinite
companion, meaning that both positive and negative values can occur.
For \(\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}=\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\), the determinant extension reduces to
\begin{equation*}
\eqfunc{scalar}(\cherm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
=\adjf{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
=\detf{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}.
\end{equation*}
Only the Hermitian pairing is being used here as the quantum bra-ket pairing.
The equal-argument determinant is the algebraic quadratic form used in
factorization identities, and the companion pairing is algebraically
well-defined but does not carry an independent standard quantum
interpretation here.

For the spatial bracket already defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:adjoint-paravector-braket}, an operator \(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\) is
Hermitian on the chosen fields and
boundary conditions when
\[
\langle\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}\mid\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\rangle
=\langle\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}\mid\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\rangle.
\]
For left multiplication by a fixed paravector \(\pv{Z}\), moving the
operator to the first field replaces it by \(\herm{\pv{Z}}\), because
\(\herm{(\herm{\pv{Z}}\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}})}=\herm{\pv{\phi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{Z}\).
For an operator that multiplies at each position, \(\Hop\) supplies the
Hermitian operation already defined by the algebra. For a differential
operator, the allowed set of fields, called its domain, must also be stated.
Conditions imposed at the edge of the spatial region or at infinite
distance are called boundary conditions
\cite{greiner2000,sakurai2017}. Integration by parts must
produce no remaining boundary contribution before the differential operator
can be called Hermitian under the spatial bracket. Nonscalar parts of
unrestricted or differently ordered products remain available as algebraic
results, but an interpretation as a current, transported amount, or
spin-axis imbalance must be established separately for the chosen operator
and field equation. For a fixed-projector state and a left-acting operator that
preserves it, the three-factor product is already restricted to the fixed
selector as shown above.

\noindent\textbf{Generalized operator products.}
The following identities are algebraic. Operator composition on a paravector-valued state is described by them, but no physical spin is assigned by them alone.

For operator-valued components, reduction of the commutator to the pure
cross-product law of Eq.\ \eqref{eq:paravector-commutator} no longer occurs.
The already defined extraction functions are retained so that no additional
component names are needed. The state is acted on by the extracted component
operators to its left, and those operators commute with the fixed basis
elements. With dot and cross products taken componentwise and in the written
operator order,
\begin{align}
\com{\pv{\mathcal{O}}}{\pv{\mathcal{O}}'}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
&=\Bigl(
\com{\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})}
    {\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')}\notag\\
&\qquad
+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
 \cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')\notag\\
&\qquad
-\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')
 \cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
\Bigr)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\notag\\
&\quad+\Bigl(
\com{\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})}
    {\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')}\notag\\
&\qquad\qquad
+\com{\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})}
    {\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')}\notag\\
&\qquad\qquad
+i\bigl(
\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
 \times\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')\bigr)\notag\\
&\qquad\qquad
-i\bigl(
\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')
 \times\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
\bigr)\notag\\
&\qquad\Bigr)\cdot\sigv\,\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}},
\label{eq:operator-commutator-action}
\end{align}
where the scalar-vector commutators are understood componentwise. Unlike the
purely algebraic paravector commutator, both scalar and vector parts are
generally contained in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:operator-commutator-action}. Only when
all component operators mutually commute is the following simpler law
obtained:
\begin{equation*}
\com{\pv{\mathcal{O}}}{\pv{\mathcal{O}}'}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
=2i\bigl(
\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
\times\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')
\bigr)\cdot\sigv\,\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}.
\end{equation*}

The matching anticommutator is
\begin{align}
\acom{\pv{\mathcal{O}}}{\pv{\mathcal{O}}'}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
&=(\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\mathcal{O}}'+\pv{\mathcal{O}}'\pv{\mathcal{O}})\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\notag\\
&=\Bigl(
\acom{\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})}
     {\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')}\notag\\
&\qquad
+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
 \cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')\notag\\
&\qquad
+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')
 \cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
\Bigr)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\notag\\
&\quad+\Bigl(
\acom{\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})}
     {\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')}\notag\\
&\qquad\qquad
+\acom{\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})}
     {\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')}\notag\\
&\qquad\qquad
+i\bigl(
\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
 \times\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')\bigr)\notag\\
&\qquad\qquad
+i\bigl(
\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}}')
 \times\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\mathcal{O}})
\bigr)\notag\\
&\qquad\Bigr)\cdot\sigv\,\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}},
\label{eq:operator-anticommutator-action}
\end{align}
so that
\begin{align*}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}\pv{\mathcal{O}}'
&=\tfrac12\bigl(\acom{\pv{\mathcal{O}}}{\pv{\mathcal{O}}'}+\com{\pv{\mathcal{O}}}{\pv{\mathcal{O}}'}\bigr).
\end{align*}
This is the natural companion to the operator commutator formula. A relation
between measurement spreads can be formed from it only after the allowed
states, the averaging rule, Hermitian operators, and a shared set of fields
on which every operator product exists have been specified. No such physical
statement is obtained from this algebraic identity alone.

\noindent\textbf{Wave-function spaces under the operators.}
A space is said to be closed under an operator when applying that operator
to any permitted member produces another member of the same space. The
action on the candidate spaces is displayed by the same algebra:
\begin{align}
\pv{\partial}:\C&\to\CPspace,\notag\\
\pv{\partial}:
\left\{\vct{V}\cdot\sigv\ \middle|\ \vct{V}\in\C^3\right\}
&\to\CPspace,\notag\\
\pv{\partial}:\CPspace&\to\CPspace,\notag\\
\pv{\partial}:\CPspace\pv{\Pi}(+)
&\to\CPspace\pv{\Pi}(+).
\label{eq:carrier-closure}
\end{align}
Indeed,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{\partial}\,\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\
&\qquad=\partial_t\,\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
+\bigl(
\gradv\,\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
\bigr)\cdot\sigv,\\
&\pv{\partial}\bigl(
\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\cdot\sigv
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\gradv\cdot\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+\partial_t\,\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
\cdot\sigv\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+i\bigl(
\gradv\times\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
\bigr)\cdot\sigv.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The full complex-paravector space \(\CPspace\) is the smallest algebra
containing the scalar and vector channels, but the projected fixed-projector
space is smaller.
Two complex amplitudes are contained in
\(\CPspace\pv{\Pi}(+)\), selected by a fixed right factor, and
this space is preserved because every differential and potential operator is
applied on the left. The full first-order paravector derivative does not
preserve the scalar subspace, whereas scalar second-order combinations such
as \(\Box+m^2\) do. A
pure-vector specialization is
algebraically meaningful, but it is not preserved by generic first-order
paravector action. A field equation that preserves the pure-vector form is
therefore required before that form is assigned a physical use.
A real-paravector specialization is not a
general quantum wave-function space because it is not preserved by
multiplication by the
unit-magnitude complex phase factor \(\exp(i\theta)\).

With those algebraic preliminaries fixed, a wave-function space is chosen first,
followed by the equation and operators under which it is preserved, and then
by the products from which measured quantities are defined. Those products
need not take values in the original wave-function space; scalar, vector, or
paravector quantities may be obtained.

\noindent\textbf{Scalar wave-function space.}
The Klein--Gordon field is the complex scalar
\begin{equation*}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\in\C.
\end{equation*}
For a chosen nonzero
real axis vector \(\vct{V}\), the unit direction is
\(\vct{u}:=\vct{V}/\|\vct{V}\|\) and the corresponding projector is
\(\pv{\Pi}(\pm)\) from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:projectors}. Multiplication by this
projector would take \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\) out of the scalar space, so the axis projectors are
not scalar-wave spin projectors. The positive quantity \(|\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}|^2\) is not
the time component of the relativistic conserved current. For \(m>0\), the
mixed current is defined in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:kg-mixed-current}. The following
relations are obtained by direct differentiation:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&2m\,\partial_t\rho\bigl(
\phi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=i(E^2\phi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}})^*
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad-i\phi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}^*
E^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&2m\,\gradv\cdot\vct{j}\bigl(
\phi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=i\phi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}^*
\vct{P}^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad-i(\vct{P}^2\phi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}})^*
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Equation \eqref{eq:kg-continuity-proof} is established by addition of the
lines and application of the two Klein--Gordon equations. For one
constant-potential plane wave, meaning one term with a single frequency and
wave vector,
\[
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}=c\exp(-i\omega t+i\vct{k}\cdot\vct{r}),
\]
where \(c\) is a constant complex amplitude, the component check is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&E\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
=(\omega-qV)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&\vct{P}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
=(\vct{k}-q\vct{A})\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&(\omega-qV)^2=(\vct{k}-q\vct{A})^2+m^2,\\
&\rho\bigl(
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\frac{\omega-qV}{m}|c|^2,\\
&\vct{j}\bigl(
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\frac{\vct{k}-q\vct{A}}{m}|c|^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Every potential and current sign is thereby verified.

The free field is obtained by setting \(q=0\). The integrated pairing in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:kg-pairing} is positive after restriction to the
positive-frequency part, meaning the solutions whose individual time factors
are \(\exp(-i\omega t)\) with \(\omega>0\), although its local density need
not be positive at every position for a sum of such solutions. That part of the
solution space must be preserved by a scalar measurement operator. Such an
operator represents a measured quantity and must be Hermitian under this
pairing so that its averages are real \cite{sakurai2017}. The split is
preserved for the free field,
or for a background that does not change with time and for which a separate
positive-energy space has been established. A single frequency split need
not be preserved by a general time-dependent electromagnetic background.
Accordingly, no local position-probability rule is claimed for the
one-particle Klein--Gordon field.

\noindent\textbf{Projected two-block wave-function space.}
For the ordered pair of projected blocks, the Hermitian pairing is
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-pairing}. A general linear operator on such pairs is
defined by its action
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{O}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2})\\[-0.2em]
&\quad:=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{\mathcal{O}}(1,1)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}
 +\pv{\mathcal{O}}(1,2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2},\\
&\pv{\mathcal{O}}(2,1)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}
 +\pv{\mathcal{O}}(2,2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}
\end{aligned}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
where the four component operators act from the left and preserve the fixed
right projector. The operator pairing defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-operator-pairing} is then expanded explicitly as
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\langle\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left}}\mid
\mathcal{O}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right}})\rangle\\
&=2\iiint\eqfunc{scalar}\!\Big(
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left},1}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}(1,1)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right},1}\\
&\qquad
+\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left},1}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}(1,2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right},2}\\
&\qquad
+\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left},2}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}(2,1)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right},1}\\
&\qquad
+\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left},2}}
\pv{\mathcal{O}}(2,2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right},2}
\Big)\,\dd r_1\,\dd r_2\,\dd r_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
By Eq.\ \eqref{eq:hermitian-column-pairing}, the ordinary normalization of
each two-amplitude block is provided by the factor \(2\). Without the fixed
right projector, two independent copies of every displayed Dirac and Pauli
equation are produced by decomposition into the two complementary
fixed-projector spaces.
The conserved density and current in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-current} are
supplied by the coupled first-order equation rather than by the vector part
of a generic Hermitian product. Equation
\eqref{eq:dirac-expectation} is the corresponding complete two-block
measurement rule.

\noindent\textbf{Pure-vector field space.}
The electromagnetic field \(\pv{F}\) is a pure-vector complex paravector.
That pure-vector space is not preserved under left multiplication by the
projectors \(\pv{\Pi}(\pm)\) for the same chosen unit direction, so this
two-projector split does not separate the independent electromagnetic field
orientations. A free massless electromagnetic wave has two independent field
orientations perpendicular to its direction of travel. The
field square that is unchanged by the stated transforms and the
observer-dependent energy and energy-flow paravector are the products already
derived in the electromagnetic section. A generic Hermitian-product bracket
is not substituted for those field quantities.

Thus spin is never fixed by component count alone. The wave-function space,
transformation law, field equation, restrictions, and products used to
calculate measured quantities must agree. For projected Pauli and Dirac
fields, the positive wave-function pairing is supplied by \(\Hop\). The scalar
Klein--Gordon field instead uses the conserved pairing defined above. The
determinant used in factorization is supplied by the star--\(\Hop\) composition,
and the pure conjugate pairing remains an algebraic companion without an
additional physical interpretation.

\subsubsection{Spin-0 Wave Mechanics}
The scalar relativistic wave operator is motivated by the classical
energy--momentum identity developed in the kinematic section. The conventional
name ``mass shell'' refers here only to the relation
\(E^2-\vct{P}^2=m^2\) obeyed by a free particle of mass \(m\).
Replacement of classical energy and momentum by differential operators is
the standard quantization prescription \cite{greiner2000} and is an
additional physical input, not a consequence of the determinant identity
alone. Here \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\in\C\) is a complex scalar field,
\(m\in\R\), and the differential conventions from
Section \ref{sec:differential} are used for the free-particle operators. The
mass-shell identity is
\begin{equation*}
\detf{m\pv{U}}=m^2.
\end{equation*}
Its scalar form is
\begin{equation*}
-E^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2=0.
\end{equation*}
For a free particle,
\begin{equation*}
E=i\partial_t,
\qquad
\vct{P}=-i\gradv,
\end{equation*}
and equivalently
\begin{equation*}
E+\vct{P}\cdot\sigv=i\conj{\pv{\partial}}.
\end{equation*}
Acting on a scalar field \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\), the operator chain is
\begin{align*}
(-E^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}
&=\bigl(-(i\partial_t)^2-\gradv^2+m^2\bigr)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\notag\\
&=\bigl(\partial_t^2-\gradv^2+m^2\bigr)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\notag\\
&=(\pv{\partial}\conj{\pv{\partial}}+m^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\notag\\
&=(\Box+m^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}.
\end{align*}
Thus the free spin-0 relativistic wave equation is
\begin{equation*}
(\Box+m^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}=0
\qquad\text{Klein--Gordon equation}.
\end{equation*}
This is the scalar wave equation recorded in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:kg-free}. For
one plane-wave factor, meaning one complex exponential with one frequency
and one spatial wave vector, the relation
\(\omega^2=\vct{k}^2+m^2\) fixes the allowed frequency \(\omega\) for each
spatial wave vector \(\vct{k}\). The conventional name for this
frequency--wave-vector relation is the dispersion relation. The later
positive/negative-frequency split follows from the same operator identity.

\subsubsection{Positive and Negative Frequency Parts}
The separation by the sign of the time frequency is one of the central
interpretive steps in the free scalar theory. On a stated spatial region and
for fields on which the positive square-root operator is defined, the
frequency function from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:frequency-operator}
is given by
\begin{align*}
\Omega
&=\sqrt{m^2-\gradv^2}\notag\\
&=\sqrt{\detf{\pv{m+\sigv\cdot\gradv}}}.
\end{align*}
Accordingly,
\begin{align*}
\Box+m^2
&=\partial_t^2+\Omega^2\notag\\
&=\partial_t^2+(m^2-\gradv^2)\notag\\
&=(\partial_t-i\Omega)(\partial_t+i\Omega)\notag\\
&=(\partial_t+i\Omega)(\partial_t-i\Omega).
\end{align*}
The two frequency-part definitions in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:frequency-split} are
then restated as
\begin{align*}
(\partial_t\pm i\Omega)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_split}}(\pm)&=0,\\
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_split}}(t,\pm)
&=\exp(\mp i\Omega t)
  \left.\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_split}}(t,\pm)\right|_{t=0},\\
\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}
&=\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_split}}(+)+\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_split}}(-).
\end{align*}
Here the parenthesized sign labels frequency and is not the sign of a
spin-axis part. The latter sign is introduced only after its unit axis has
been specified in Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:magnetic-axis} and
\eqref{eq:pauli-spin-decomposition}.
When \(\Omega^{-1}\) exists, the two parts of a Klein--Gordon solution at any
time can equivalently be extracted as
\begin{equation*}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_split}}(\pm)
=\frac12\left(
\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\pm i\Omega^{-1}\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}
\right).
\end{equation*}
For a massless field, the spatially constant term with
\(\vct{k}=\vct{0}\) is treated separately. The Klein--Gordon pairing has
opposite sign on a single negative-frequency wave and
is not a positive norm on the combined solution space. In a quantized
relativistic field the particle--antiparticle interpretation is supplied by
the field-operator construction, not by reinterpreting this one-particle
density alone \cite{greiner2000,peskin1995}.

\subsubsection{Fourier Meaning of the Frequency Operator}
The square root \(\Omega\) is most transparent after the field has been
written as a sum of plane waves. This is the spatial Fourier decomposition:
each term is called a mode and is labeled by one wave vector
\(\vct{k}(n)\) and one frequency \(\omega(n)\)
\cite{greiner2000}. The operator is called nonlocal because its value at one
position generally depends on field values at other positions, even though
its action on each plane wave is simple. With the stated phase convention,
the field and its modes are written schematically as
\begin{align*}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}&=\sum_{n} c(n)f(n),\\
f(n)&=\exp\!\bigl(-i\omega(n)t+i\vct{k}(n)\cdot\vct{r}\bigr).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align*}
\Box\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}
&=\sum_{n}(\vct{k}(n)^{2}-\omega(n)^{2})c(n)f(n),\\
(\Box+m^{2})\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}
&=\sum_{n}(\vct{k}(n)^{2}+m^{2}-\omega(n)^{2})c(n)f(n),\\
\Omega\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}
&=\sum_n\sqrt{m^2+\vct{k}(n)^2}\,c(n)f(n).
\end{align*}
For modes satisfying the Klein--Gordon frequency--wave-vector relation,
\begin{equation*}
\vct{k}(n)^{2}+m^{2}=\omega(n)^{2},
\qquad
\omega(n)=\pm\sqrt{\vct{k}(n)^{2}+m^{2}},
\end{equation*}
and each spatial mode is multiplied by its positive energy magnitude by
\(\Omega\). The set of frequencies used in the sum is therefore restricted
to the two displayed values for every \(\vct{k}(n)\).
The only square-root operator needed in the frequency split is thereby
defined. No choice between the possible square-root values of \(\Box\) is
needed.

\subsubsection{Klein--Gordon with Potential Fields}
The charged-particle Lagrangian, a function from which the classical motion
is obtained by the stationary-action rule, is taken as the starting point.
Under that rule, a small variation of the physical path produces no
first-order change in the time integral of the Lagrangian
\cite{jackson1998}. The scalar theory with electromagnetic potential is
thereby kept in one self-contained place:
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal L=-\frac{m}{\gamma}-q(V-\vct{v}\cdot\vct{A}).
\end{equation*}
Here \(E\) denotes the energy operator, not the electric field \(\vct{E}\).
The classical Lagrangian first supplies total energy and canonical momentum,
where ``canonical'' names the momentum that includes the potential term.
After the standard differential replacement, subtraction of the potential
terms gives the kinetic, or gauge-covariant, energy and momentum operators
\begin{equation*}
E=i\partial_t-qV,
\qquad
\vct{P}=-i\gradv-q\vct{A}.
\end{equation*}
Here ``kinetic'' means that the potential contribution has been removed so
that these quantities correspond to the particle's mechanical energy and
momentum. ``Gauge-covariant'' means that their action on the phase-shifted
field changes by the same overall phase, as verified below. This differential
replacement, together with the stated potential substitution, is the
standard quantization input \cite{greiner2000}.

Acting on a field \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\) whose displayed derivatives exist, the ordinary
derivative product rules needed below are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t(V\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}})\\
&\qquad=(\partial_tV)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+V\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&\partial_m(A_n\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}})\\
&\qquad=(\partial_mA_n)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+A_n\partial_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The following expansions are obtained:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&E^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=E\bigl(
i\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
-qV\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&-\partial_t^2-iq\,\partial_tV\\[-0.2em]
&-2iqV\partial_t+q^2V^2
\end{aligned}
\right)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&\vct{P}^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=\vct{P}\cdot\bigl(
-i\gradv\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
-q\vct{A}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&-\gradv^2+iq\,\gradv\cdot\vct{A}\\[-0.2em]
&+2iq\,\vct{A}\cdot\gradv+q^2\vct{A}^2
\end{aligned}
\right)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

The required identities are
\begin{align*}
\Box&=\partial_t^2-\gradv^2,\\
\pv{\Phi}&=V+\vct{A}\cdot\sigv,\\
S&=\partial_tV+\gradv\cdot\vct{A},\\
\detf{\pv{\partial}}&=\Box,\\
\detf{\pv{\Phi}}&=V^2-\vct{A}^2.
\end{align*}
Here \(\detf{\pv{\partial}}=\pv{\partial}\conj{\pv{\partial}}\) is valid because the
coordinate derivatives commute. The coupled energy and momentum operators
below do not commute and therefore remain explicit ordered products rather
than determinants.
The Klein--Gordon equation with the stated electromagnetic potential is
obtained in explicit form by substitution of the operator expansions:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&0=(-E^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\Box+iq(\partial_tV+\gradv\cdot\vct{A})\\[-0.2em]
&+2iq(V\partial_t+\vct{A}\cdot\gradv)\\[-0.2em]
&-q^2(V^2-\vct{A}^2)+m^2
\end{aligned}
\right)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\Box+iqS-q^2\,\detf{\pv{\Phi}}+m^2\\[-0.2em]
&+2iq(V\partial_t+\vct{A}\cdot\gradv)
\end{aligned}
\right)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

For the gauge-shifted potential and components defined in
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:gauge}, \eqref{eq:gauge-scalar-potential}, and
\eqref{eq:gauge-vector-potential},
\[
\begin{aligned}
V'&=V+\partial_t\lambda,\\
\vct{A}'&=\vct{A}-\gradv\lambda,\\
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}'
&=\exp(-iq\lambda)\,\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The real function \(\lambda(t,\vct{r})\) is the gauge parameter: it labels
different potential functions that produce the same electric and magnetic
fields. The accompanying phase change of \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\) ensures that these
different descriptions give the same coupled equation \cite{jackson1998}.
The ordinary product rule is obeyed by the central scalar phase. Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&(i\partial_t-qV')\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}'\\
&\qquad=\exp(-iq\lambda)\,
(i\partial_t-qV)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&(-i\gradv-q\vct{A}')\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}'\\
&\qquad=\exp(-iq\lambda)\,
(-i\gradv-q\vct{A})\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Equation \eqref{eq:kg-gauge} is established by a second application of either
relation. Thus the coupled Klein--Gordon equation retains the same form after
the linked potential and phase changes. Substitution in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:kg-mixed-current} also cancels the phase from both current
components.

The electromagnetic fields and commutators are incorporated through
\begin{align*}
\vct{E}&=-\partial_t\vct{A}-\gradv V,\\
\vct{B}&=\gradv\times\vct{A},\\
\pv{F}&=(\vct{E}+i\vct{B})\cdot\sigv,\qquad
\conj{\pv{F}}=(-\vct{E}+i\vct{B})\cdot\sigv,
\end{align*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\com{E}{P_m}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=(i\partial_t-qV)
\left(
\begin{aligned}
&-i\partial_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&-qA_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\end{aligned}
\right)\\
&\qquad\quad-(-i\partial_m-qA_m)
\left(
\begin{aligned}
&i\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&-qV\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\end{aligned}
\right)\\
&\qquad=-iq\bigl(
(\partial_tA_m)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+A_m\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad\quad-iq\bigl(
(\partial_mV)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+V\partial_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad\quad+iqV\partial_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+iqA_m\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=-iq(\partial_tA_m+\partial_mV)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=iqE_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\com{P_m}{P_n}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=(-i\partial_m-qA_m)
\left(
\begin{aligned}
&-i\partial_n\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&-qA_n\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\end{aligned}
\right)\\
&\qquad\quad-(-i\partial_n-qA_n)
\left(
\begin{aligned}
&-i\partial_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\[-0.2em]
&-qA_m\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\end{aligned}
\right)\\
&\qquad=iq(\partial_mA_n-\partial_nA_m)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Hence
\begin{equation*}
\com{E}{\vct{P}}=iq\vct{E},
\qquad
\vct{P}\times\vct{P}=iq\vct{B}.
\end{equation*}
The following ordered products are obtained by substitution of those commutators:
\begin{align*}
(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})(E-\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
&=E^2-\vct{P}^2\notag\\
&\qquad+\sigv\cdot\bigl(-\com{E}{\vct{P}}-i\,\vct{P}\times\vct{P}\bigr)\notag\\
&=E^2-\vct{P}^2-iq\pv{F},\\
(E-\sigv\cdot\vct{P})(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
&=E^2-\vct{P}^2\notag\\
&\qquad+\sigv\cdot\bigl(\com{E}{\vct{P}}-i\,\vct{P}\times\vct{P}\bigr)\notag\\
&=E^2-\vct{P}^2-iq\conj{\pv{F}}.
\end{align*}
Since
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P}
=i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi},\\
&E-\sigv\cdot\vct{P}
=i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad=(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})^{*\Hop},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
the two ordered star--\(\Hop\) products are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&(E\pm\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
(E\pm\sigv\cdot\vct{P})^{*\Hop}\\
&\qquad=E^2-\vct{P}^2\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad
+\sigv\cdot\bigl(
\mp\,\com{E}{\vct{P}}-i\,\vct{P}\times\vct{P}
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Unlike the determinant function defined for commuting complex
coefficients, these operator products retain a vector part.  Operator ordering
therefore matters, and the two first-order products remain distinct.
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
-(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})^{*\Hop}
&=(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}}),\\
-(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})^{*\Hop}(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
&=(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi}).
\end{align*}
The same factorizations can be rewritten directly in derivative form:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})(E-\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=-(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=-\Box\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
-iq\,\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bigl(
\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad\quad
-iq\,\pv{\Phi}\,\pv{\partial}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+q^2\,\detf{\pv{\Phi}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=-\Box\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
-iqS\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad
+q^2\,\detf{\pv{\Phi}}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad-2iq(V\partial_t+\vct{A}\cdot\gradv)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad-iq\pv{F}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&(E-\sigv\cdot\vct{P})(E+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=-(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=-\Box\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
-iq\,\pv{\partial}\bigl(
\pv{\Phi}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\
&\qquad\quad
-iq\,\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\,\conj{\pv{\partial}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+q^2\,\detf{\pv{\Phi}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=-\Box\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
-iqS\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad
+q^2\,\detf{\pv{\Phi}}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad-2iq(V\partial_t+\vct{A}\cdot\gradv)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad-iq\conj{\pv{F}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bigl(
\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)
+\pv{\Phi}\,\pv{\partial}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=S\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+2(V\partial_t+\vct{A}\cdot\gradv)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad+\pv{F}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}},\\
&\pv{\partial}\bigl(
\pv{\Phi}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)
+\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\,\conj{\pv{\partial}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=S\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+2(V\partial_t+\vct{A}\cdot\gradv)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad+\conj{\pv{F}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
and their difference isolates the electric contribution:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\left[
\begin{aligned}
&\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bigl(
\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\[-0.2em]
&+\pv{\Phi}\,\pv{\partial}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\end{aligned}
\right]\\
&\quad-\left[
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{\partial}\bigl(
\pv{\Phi}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\bigr)\\[-0.2em]
&+\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\,\conj{\pv{\partial}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
\end{aligned}
\right]\\
&\qquad=\bigl(
\conj{\pv{\partial}\pv{\Phi}}-\pv{\partial}\pv{\Phi}
\bigr)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=(\pv{F}-\conj{\pv{F}})
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=2(\vct{E}\cdot\sigv)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

From the definitions in
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:energy-operator} and \eqref{eq:momentum-operator},
\eqref{eq:partial-operator}, \eqref{eq:partial-conjugate}, and
\eqref{eq:F-Phi}, the scalar Klein--Gordon operator can therefore be recovered
from either ordered first-order product:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&0=(-E^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\Box+iqS-q^2\,\detf{\pv{\Phi}}+m^2\\[-0.2em]
&+2iq(V\partial_t+\vct{A}\cdot\gradv)
\end{aligned}
\right)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad-iq\pv{F}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+m^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad=(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&\qquad\quad-iq\conj{\pv{F}}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
+m^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The following identities are satisfied by the ordered first-order products:
\begin{align*}
(E\pm\sigv\cdot\vct{P})(E\mp\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
&=E^2-(\sigv\cdot\vct{P})^2\mp\,\com{E}{\sigv\cdot\vct{P}}\notag\\
&=E^2-\vct{P}^2-iq(\pm\vct{E}+i\vct{B})\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
The following equation is therefore recovered by taking the scalar part:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\eqfunc{scalar}\!\bigl(
(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
\bigr)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\\
&+m^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The Klein--Gordon operator itself is reproduced only by the scalar part; the full
ordered products retain the non-scalar field terms \(-iq\pv{F}\) and
\(-iq\conj{\pv{F}}\) that distinguish the two factorizations. Explicitly,
\[
-iq\pv{F}=(-iq\vct{E}+q\vct{B})\cdot\sigv,
\qquad
-iq\conj{\pv{F}}=(iq\vct{E}+q\vct{B})\cdot\sigv.
\]
These remaining terms are not new components of the scalar equation. They
become the standard spin--field terms in the two ordered Dirac squares below
\cite{greiner2000}.

\subsubsection{Spin-\texorpdfstring{$\frac12$}{1/2} Wave Mechanics}
The Dirac field is treated intrinsically as the ordered pair
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}:=(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}).
\end{equation*}
For clarity, the fixed axis and projector from
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:fixed-column-axis} and
\eqref{eq:fixed-column-carrier} are restated in the same order:
\begin{align*}
\vct{u}&=\vct{e}_3=\vthree{0}{0}{1},\\
\pv{\Pi}(+)&=\frac12(1+\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)
=\frac12(1+\pv{\sigma}_3).
\end{align*}
One Dirac field is selected by requiring both paravector blocks to satisfy
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},n}=\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},n}\pv{\Pi}(+),
\qquad n\in\{1,2\}.
\end{equation*}
All differential and potential factors are applied on the left, so this
restriction is preserved by associativity.

The projected algebra is spanned by the fixed projector and the previously
defined ladder element \(\pv{a}(-)\). Consequently, the pair is specified by
four complex scalar amplitudes \(a,b,c,d\):
\begin{align*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}
&=a\pv{\Pi}(+)+b\pv{a}(-),\\
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}
&=c\pv{\Pi}(+)+d\pv{a}(-).
\end{align*}
This form is obtained from the following right-action identities:
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\Pi}(+)^2=\pv{\Pi}(+),
\qquad
\pv{a}(-)\pv{\Pi}(+)=\pv{a}(-).
\end{equation*}
Thus two complex amplitudes are carried intrinsically by each projected
paravector.

Before the electromagnetic field is introduced, the first-order system is
written directly as the two coupled paravector equations
\begin{align*}
i\pv{\partial}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}&=0,\\
i\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}&=0.
\end{align*}
The following reductions are obtained by application of the opposite
first-order factor:
\begin{align*}
i\conj{\pv{\partial}}
\bigl(i\pv{\partial}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}\bigr)
&=-(\Box+m^2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}=0,\\
i\pv{\partial}
\bigl(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}\bigr)
&=-(\Box+m^2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}=0.
\end{align*}
Thus the Klein--Gordon equation is required for both blocks.

For a free plane wave proportional to
\(\exp(-iEt+i\vct{p}\cdot\vct{r})\), the following scalar component
equations are obtained by substitution of the amplitude decomposition:
\begin{align*}
(E-p_3)c-(p_1-ip_2)d&=ma,\\
-(p_1+ip_2)c+(E+p_3)d&=mb,\\
(E+p_3)a+(p_1-ip_2)b&=mc,\\
(p_1+ip_2)a+(E-p_3)b&=md.
\end{align*}
These are the standard four first-order Dirac component equations
\cite{dirac1928,greiner2000}. The relation
\(E^2=\vct{p}^2+m^2\) is obtained from their ordered products. With a
potential present, the operator-valued component equations in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-four-components} are obtained from the same algebraic
decomposition.

Their transformation law is determined by requiring the two equations to
retain the same form rather than by counting components. For a constant
determinant-one transform \(\pv{T}\), with
\(\pv{X}'\) given by Eq.\ \eqref{eq:general-event-congruence}, the transformed
blocks are assigned the laws from Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-transform} and
\eqref{eq:dirac-transform-lower}:
\begin{align*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}'(t',\vct{r}')
&=\pv{T}^{\Hop}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}(t,\vct{r}),\\
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}'(t',\vct{r}')
&=\pv{T}^{-1}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}(t,\vct{r}).
\end{align*}
The corresponding derivative and potential laws are
\begin{align}
\pv{\partial}'&=\pv{T}^{\Hop}\pv{\partial}\pv{T},
\label{eq:dirac-transform-derivative}\\
\conj{\pv{\partial}'}
&=\pv{T}^{-1}\conj{\pv{\partial}}\conj{\pv{T}},
\label{eq:dirac-transform-conjugate-derivative}\\
\pv{\Phi}'&=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{\Phi}\pv{T}^*,
\label{eq:dirac-transform-potential}\\
\conj{\pv{\Phi}'}
&=\pv{T}^{\Hop}\conj{\pv{\Phi}}\pv{T}.
\label{eq:dirac-transform-conjugate-potential}
\end{align}
For a spatial rotor, the same left factor \(\pv{R}^{\Hop}\) is assigned
to both blocks. For a boost, \(\pv{L}\) is assigned to the first block and
\(\conj{\pv{L}}\) to the second. Since
\(\detf{\pv{T}}=1\), the identities
\(\pv{T}\pv{T}^{-1}=1\) and
\(\conj{\pv{T}}\pv{T}^{\Hop}=1\) are used in the following explicit
substitutions:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&(i\pv{\partial}'-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}'})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}'
\\
&\qquad=\pv{T}^{\Hop}
(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}\\
&\qquad=m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}',\\
&(i\conj{\pv{\partial}'}-q\pv{\Phi}')
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}'
\\
&\qquad=\pv{T}^{-1}
(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}\\
&\qquad=m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}'.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus both coupled equations retain the same form after the transformation.
Because all transform factors are applied from the left, the fixed right projector
is preserved.

The real current paravector defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-current-paravector} is expanded as
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{J}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})\\
&\qquad=2\left(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}}
\right.\\
&\qquad\quad\left.
+\conj{\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}}
\bigr)}
\right)\\
&\qquad=\rho(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})
+\vct{j}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})\cdot\sigv.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The following transformation is obtained from
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-transform} and \eqref{eq:dirac-transform-lower}
in each product:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}'
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}'}
\\
&\qquad=\pv{T}^{-1}
  \bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
  \herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}}
  (\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop},\\
&\conj{\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}'
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}'}
\bigr)}
\\
&\qquad=\pv{T}^{-1}
  \conj{\bigl(
  \bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}
  \herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}}
  \bigr)}
  (\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Here
\(\conj{(\pv{T}^{\Hop})}=\pv{T}^{-1}\) and
\(\pv{T}^*=(\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}\) are obtained from
\(\detf{\pv{T}}=1\). Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{J}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'(t',\vct{r}')
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\pv{T}^{-1}\pv{J}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}(t,\vct{r})
\bigr)
(\pv{T}^{-1})^{\Hop}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
so the current is transformed by the same two-sided rule as a real event
paravector.
For the amplitude decomposition in the fixed projected algebra, the
component form is
\begin{align*}
\rho(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})
&=|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2+|d|^2,\\
\vct{j}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})
&=
\vthreeeq
{2\,\eqfunc{real}(c^*d-a^*b)}
{2\,\eqfunc{img}(c^*d-a^*b)}
{|c|^2-|d|^2-|a|^2+|b|^2}.
\end{align*}
These are the conventional Dirac density and current components. The two
uncancelled block balances are Eq.\
\eqref{eq:dirac-continuity-components}; by their addition,
\(\partial_t\rho(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})
+\gradv\cdot\vct{j}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})=0\)
is established. In particular,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\vct{u}\cdot\vct{j}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})\\
&=2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}}
(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\right)\\
&\quad
-2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}}
(\vct{u}\cdot\sigv)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}
\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
so this is the local Hermitian-pairing density for the two-block velocity
operator along the unit direction \(\vct{u}\), where
\(\vct{u}^2=1\). The action of this directional operator is
\(-\vct{u}\cdot\sigv\) on
\(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}\) and
\(+\vct{u}\cdot\sigv\) on
\(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}\). Its spatial integral divided by
\(\langle\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}
\mid\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}\rangle\) is the corresponding average velocity
component along \(\vct{u}\). The constant-time density is the time entry measured in one chosen
reference frame. It is not a Lorentz scalar, meaning a number unchanged by a
Lorentz transformation.

For the free system with \(q=0\) and \(m>0\), the following result is obtained
by solving the first equation
for the first block and substituting into the second:
\begin{align*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}&=\frac{i\pv{\partial}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}}{m},\\
0&=i\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}\notag\\
&=-\frac{\conj{\pv{\partial}}\pv{\partial}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}}{m}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}\notag\\
&=-\frac{(\Box+m^2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}}{m}.
\end{align*}
Hence the second block is a Klein--Gordon solution, and the first block is
obtained from it by one first-order derivative.

Conversely, a scalar Klein--Gordon solution \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\) and constant
projected amplitude elements
\(\pv{a},\pv{b}\in\CPspace\pv{\Pi}(+)\) are taken. The
corresponding ordered pair is defined intrinsically by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}&:=\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\pv{a}
+\frac{i}{m}(\pv{\partial}\psi_{\eqtext{KG}})\pv{b},\\
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}&:=\frac{i}{m}(\conj{\pv{\partial}}\psi_{\eqtext{KG}})\pv{a}
+\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\pv{b},\\
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}&:=(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dirac-klein-gordon-lift}
\end{equation}
Because the constants occur on the right, only \(\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\) is acted on by the
derivatives. The following reductions are obtained by direct substitution
into the two free Dirac equations:
\begin{align*}
i\pv{\partial}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}
&=-\frac1m(\Box+m^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\,\pv{a}=0,\\
i\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}
&=-\frac1m(\Box+m^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\,\pv{b}=0.
\end{align*}
The spin-axis choice is carried by the constants \(\pv{a}\) and \(\pv{b}\);
it is not carried by a bare scalar solution alone. For example, the following
ordered pair is obtained with \(\pv{a}=\pv{\Pi}(+)\) and
\(\pv{b}=0\):
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}=
\left(
\psi_{\eqtext{KG}}\pv{\Pi}(+),
\frac{i}{m}(\conj{\pv{\partial}}\psi_{\eqtext{KG}})
 \pv{\Pi}(+)
\right).
\end{equation*}
A Klein--Gordon solution satisfying
\(E^2-\vct{p}^2=m^2\) is thereby converted into a field in the fixed
projected Dirac space. The
two-input presentation is symmetric but need not be one-to-one. For each
single plane wave satisfying the displayed energy--momentum relation, a
nonzero \(\pv{b}\) together with
\(\pv{a}=-(E-\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)\pv{b}/m\) is sent to the zero pair.
The same Dirac plane wave can therefore be produced by different input
pairs. For a fixed plane wave with \(m>0\), the two spin-axis possibilities
are already produced from one projected input block. Four new spin
possibilities are not introduced by the symbols \(\pv{a}\) and \(\pv{b}\).

\subsubsection{Dirac with Potential Field}
The field is again treated as the ordered pair
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}
:=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1},\\
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\end{aligned}
\right).
\end{equation*}
One Dirac field remains restricted by
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},n}
=\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},n}
\pv{\Pi}(+).
\end{equation*}
Here \(n\in\{1,2\}\).
All potential and derivative factors are applied from the left, and the
fixed projected space is therefore preserved.

The pair-valued Dirac function is defined by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal D(\pv{\Phi},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})\\
&\qquad:=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1},\\
&(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\end{aligned}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dirac-pair-function}
\end{equation}
The partner function is defined by reversing only the mass sign:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal D'(\pv{\Phi},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})\\
&\qquad:=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad+m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1},\\
&(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad+m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\end{aligned}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dirac-pair-function-partner}
\end{equation}
This distinct mass-sign choice is labeled by the prime; no derivative is
denoted. In terms of Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-pair-function}, the coupled Dirac
equation is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal D(\pv{\Phi},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})=(0,0).
\end{equation*}
The following paravector equations are obtained from its two entries:
\begin{align*}
-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}+(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}})\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}&=0,\\
(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi})\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}&=0.
\end{align*}
Equivalently, their time evolution is
\begin{align*}
i\partial_t\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}
&=(qV-\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}\notag\\
&\quad+m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2},\\
i\partial_t\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
&=(qV+\sigv\cdot\vct{P})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}\notag\\
&\quad+m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}.
\end{align*}
The \(\Hop\) operation is applied to these equations, multiplication is
performed on the opposite side, and scalar parts are taken. In the fixed
projected form, the two separate balance equations are written
out in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-continuity-components}. The real potential terms
are canceled within each block, while the mass terms are equal and opposite
between the blocks. The following relation is obtained by addition of those
two equations:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\partial_t\left[
\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}
\right)\right.\\
&\left.\qquad+
\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\right)\right]\\
&+\gradv\cdot\left[
\eqfunc{vector}\!\left(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}}
\right)\right.\\
&\left.\qquad-
\eqfunc{vector}\!\left(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}
\herm{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}}
\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The stated density and current are thereby verified, and the distinction
between the vector part of a single
\(\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}}\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}\) and the
Dirac current is established. The following result is obtained by solving the
first equation for the first block and substituting into the second:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}\\
&\qquad=\frac1m(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2},\\
&0=(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}
-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}\\
&\qquad=-\frac1m\Bigl(
(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
+m^2
\Bigr)\\
&\qquad\quad{}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus a Klein--Gordon-type operator with the appropriate ordered field
coupling is satisfied by each block.

The following result is obtained by application of the mass-sign partner to
the pair returned by \(\mathcal D\):
\begin{align*}
0
&=\mathcal D'\bigl(
\pv{\Phi},\mathcal D(\pv{\Phi},\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})
\bigr)\notag\\
&=-\Bigl(
\bigl((\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})+m^2\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1},\notag\\
&\qquad\quad
\bigl((\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})+m^2\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}
\Bigr).
\end{align*}
Equivalently,
\begin{align*}
0&=\bigl((\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
(\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})+m^2\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}\notag\\
&=\bigl(-E^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2+iq\conj{\pv{F}}\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1},\\
0&=\bigl((\conj{\pv{\partial}}+iq\pv{\Phi})
(\pv{\partial}+iq\conj{\pv{\Phi}})+m^2\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}\notag\\
&=\bigl(-E^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2+iq\pv{F}\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}.
\end{align*}
Since
\[
iq\conj{\pv{F}}=(-iq\vct{E}-q\vct{B})\cdot\sigv,
\qquad
iq\pv{F}=(iq\vct{E}-q\vct{B})\cdot\sigv,
\]
the two equations are exactly the component forms in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-square-components}. Once the square is taken, the
coupled Dirac system is separated into two Klein--Gordon-type equations with the
same magnetic term and opposite electric terms, as required by the two
first-order blocks.

\subsubsection{Gauge Transform of the Dirac System}
For the real gauge parameter \(\lambda(t,\vct{r})\) defined above, the
potential in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:gauge} is
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\Phi}'=\pv{\Phi}+\conj{\pv{\partial}}\lambda.
\end{equation*}
Because \(\lambda\) is real,
\begin{equation*}
\pv{\partial}\lambda=\conj{\pv{\Phi}'-\pv{\Phi}}.
\end{equation*}
The field transformation defined in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-gauge} is
equivalently written as
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}
=\exp(iq\lambda)\,\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'.
\end{equation*}
The phase factor is a central scalar, so every commutator correction in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:paravector-leibniz} is zero. The ordinary two-term product rule
is therefore obeyed by both derivative factors:
\begin{align*}
\pv{\partial}\bigl(
\exp(iq\lambda)\,\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'
\bigr)
&=\exp(iq\lambda)
\bigl(iq\,\pv{\partial}\lambda+\pv{\partial}\bigr)\notag\\
&\qquad
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}',\\
\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bigl(
\exp(iq\lambda)\,\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'
\bigr)
&=\exp(iq\lambda)
\bigl(iq\,\conj{\pv{\partial}}\lambda
+\conj{\pv{\partial}}\bigr)\notag\\
&\qquad
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'.
\end{align*}
The two first-order factors are consequently transformed as
\begin{align*}
(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}})
\exp(iq\lambda)\notag\\
\qquad{}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}'
&=\exp(iq\lambda)
(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}'})\notag\\
&\qquad
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}',\\
(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi})
\exp(iq\lambda)\notag\\
\qquad{}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}'
&=\exp(iq\lambda)
(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi}')\notag\\
&\qquad
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}'.
\end{align*}
The following relation is therefore satisfied by the pair-valued function:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal D\bigl(
\pv{\Phi},
\exp(iq\lambda)\,\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\exp(iq\lambda)
\mathcal D\bigl(
\pv{\Phi}',\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Since the left side vanishes for the original field, the following equation
is satisfied by the transformed field:
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal D\bigl(
\pv{\Phi}',\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}}'
\bigr)=(0,0).
\end{equation*}
The phase change of the Dirac field is exactly compensated by the shift of
the potential. The same form of the coupled Dirac equation is retained for
every value of the gauge parameter.

\begin{equation*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}
=\eqfunc{scalar}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})
+\eqfunc{vector}(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}})\cdot\sigv.
\end{equation*}
A physical projected block is selected by the previously defined right

\begin{equation*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{block}}

\subsubsection{Low-Energy Spin-0 Limit}
For the scalar theory with electromagnetic potential, the kinetic operators are
\begin{equation*}
E=i\partial_t-qV,
\qquad
\vct{P}=-i\gradv-q\vct{A}.
\end{equation*}
The fast rest-mass oscillation is removed by writing
\begin{equation*}
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
=\exp(-imt)\,\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{align*}
E^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
&=\exp(-imt)(E+m)^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}},\\
\vct{P}^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}
&=\exp(-imt)\,\vct{P}^2\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}.
\end{align*}
The following result is obtained by substitution into the Klein--Gordon
equation with the stated electromagnetic potential:
\begin{align*}
0&=(-E^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_with\_potential}}\notag\\
&=\exp(-imt)\,\bigl(-(E+m)^2+\vct{P}^2+m^2\bigr)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}},
\end{align*}
hence
\begin{equation*}
0=(-E^2-2mE+\vct{P}^2)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}.
\end{equation*}
For the free theory, or more generally when \(E\) and \(\vct{P}^{\,2}\)
commute on the fields being considered, the following two solutions are
admitted by the quadratic equation:
\[
E=-m\pm\sqrt{m^2+\vct{P}^2}.
\]
The root near zero is selected by the slow positive-energy assumption:
\begin{align*}
E\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}
&=\left(-m+\sqrt{m^2+\vct{P}^2}\right)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}\notag\\
&=\left(\frac{\vct{P}^2}{2m}-\frac{\vct{P}^4}{8m^3}
+O(\vct{P}^6/m^5)\right)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}.
\end{align*}
The other root begins at \(-2m\) after the rest phase is removed and is
incompatible with the assumed slowly varying positive-energy solution.
For noncommuting or time-dependent potentials, commutators are also contained
in the higher-order terms; the leading low-energy relation remains
\begin{equation*}
E\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}
\approx \frac{\vct{P}^2}{2m}\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}.
\end{equation*}
The Schr\"odinger equation is obtained by solving for
\(i\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}\) \cite{sakurai2017}:
\begin{equation*}
i\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}
\approx\left(\frac{(-i\gradv-q\vct{A})^2}{2m}+qV\right)
\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}.
\end{equation*}
If \(\vct{A}=0\), this reduces to
\begin{equation*}
i\partial_t\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}
\approx\left(-\frac{\gradv^2}{2m}+qV\right)\psi_{\eqtext{KG\_slow}}.
\end{equation*}
The scalar low-energy limit is therefore already encoded in the relativistic Klein--Gordon theory.

\subsubsection{Low-Energy Spin-\texorpdfstring{$\frac12$}{1/2} and Pauli Limit}
The projected two-block field from
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-coupled} is used. Its two block equations are
developed here under the assumption \(m>0\):
\begin{align*}
(i\pv{\partial}-q\conj{\pv{\Phi}})\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}&=0,\\
(i\conj{\pv{\partial}}-q\pv{\Phi})\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}&=0.
\end{align*}
With the kinetic operators from
Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:energy-operator} and \eqref{eq:momentum-operator},
\begin{equation*}
E=i\partial_t-qV,
\qquad
\vct{P}=-i\gradv-q\vct{A},
\end{equation*}
the system is
\begin{align*}
(E-\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}&=0,\\
(E+\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},1}-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},2}&=0.
\end{align*}
The slow fields defined in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:slow-field-definition} are related by
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},n}
=\exp(-imt)\,\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},n}.
\end{equation*}
Since
\begin{equation*}
E\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential},n}
=\exp(-imt)(E+m)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},n},
\end{equation*}
the system is reduced to
\begin{align*}
(E+m-\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},2}
-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},1}&=0,\\
(E+m+\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},1}
-m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},2}&=0.
\end{align*}
Neither original block is small at rest. With the energy-basis combinations
from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:large-small},
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
&=\frac{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},1}
       +\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},2}}{\sqrt{2}},\\
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}}
&=\frac{\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},2}
       -\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_slow},1}}{\sqrt{2}}.
\end{align*}
The exact system is obtained by addition and subtraction of the reduced equations:
\begin{align*}
E\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
&=(\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}},\\
(E+2m)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}}
&=(\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}.
\end{align*}
Thus \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\) is the large positive-energy field, and
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}}\) is suppressed by one power of \(1/m\).
Formally,
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}}
&=(E+2m)^{-1}(\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}},\\
E\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
&=(\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)(E+2m)^{-1}
  (\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\notag\\
&=\frac{(\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)^2}{2m}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}+O(m^{-2}),
\end{align*}
where momenta small compared with \(m\), energies small compared with \(2m\),
and external fields changing little across the length \(1/m\) are assumed.
In the units used here, \(1/m\) is the reduced Compton length associated
with the particle
\cite{greiner2000}.

The magnetic term is obtained directly from the kinetic-momentum commutator:
\begin{align*}
\com{P_m}{P_n}&=iq(\partial_mA_n-\partial_nA_m),\\
\vct{P}\times\vct{P}&=iq\,\vct{B},\\
(\vct{P}\cdot\sigv)^2
&=\vct{P}^2+i(\vct{P}\times\vct{P})\cdot\sigv\notag\\
&=\vct{P}^2-q\,\vct{B}\cdot\sigv.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
E\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
=\frac{\vct{P}^2-q\,\vct{B}\cdot\sigv}{2m}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
+O(m^{-2}),
\end{equation*}
and the Pauli equation is
\begin{align*}
i\partial_t\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
&=\left(\frac{(-i\gradv-q\vct{A})^2}{2m}+qV\right)
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\notag\\
&\quad-\frac{q}{2m}(\vct{B}\cdot\sigv)
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}+O(m^{-2}).
\end{align*}
At the next order, electric-field commutators generate a coupling between
spin and motion in an electric field, conventionally called the spin--orbit
term, and a local correction proportional to
\(\gradv\cdot\vct{E}\), conventionally called the Darwin term
\cite{greiner2000,sakurai2017}. Time-dependent corrections are also
generated because \(E\) and \(\vct{P}\) do not commute. All such terms must
be retained consistently rather than
discarded from the second-order equation for one original block. The leading
spin coupling is therefore a controlled consequence of the Dirac system
\cite{pauli1927,sakurai2017}.

The Pauli current in Eq.\ \eqref{eq:pauli-current} is obtained from the same
controlled reduction. Equation \eqref{eq:large-small-current-change} gives
the intrinsic current relation
\begin{align*}
\vct{j}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_with\_potential}})
&=2\,\eqfunc{vector}\!\Bigl(
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}}}\notag\\
&\qquad
+\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}}
\Bigr),\\
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_small}}
&=\frac{\vct{P}\cdot\sigv}{2m}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}+O(m^{-2}).
\end{align*}
The sigma product and the differential product rule give each of the three
current components without leaving the projected algebra:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&j_n(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}})\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{m}\,
\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}}
P_n\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\\[-0.2em]
&+\herm{(P_n\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}})}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
\end{aligned}
\right)\\
&\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{m}\left[
\gradv\times
\eqfunc{vector}\!\left(
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}}
\right)
\right]_n
+O(m^{-2}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where \(n\in\{1,2,3\}\).
The amplitude content of this result is exposed by writing
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}&=a\pv{\Pi}(+)+b\pv{a}(-),\\
P_n\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
&=(P_n a)\pv{\Pi}(+)+(P_n b)\pv{a}(-).
\end{align*}
The projector--ladder products then give
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&2\,\eqfunc{scalar}\!\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}}
P_n\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\\[-0.2em]
&+\herm{(P_n\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}})}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
\end{aligned}
\right)\notag\\
&\qquad
=a^*P_n a+b^*P_n b\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+(P_n a)^*a+(P_n b)^*b,\\
&2\,\eqfunc{vector}\!\left(
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
\herm{\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}}
\right)\\
&\qquad=\vthreeeq
{a^*b+b^*a}
{i(b^*a-a^*b)}
{|a|^2-|b|^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The first relation supplies the three
kinetic-momentum-flow components, and the second supplies the spin-density
vector.
The first term is the kinetic-momentum flow. The second is the curl of the
spin-density vector and is conventionally called the magnetization current
\cite{sakurai2017}. This curl has zero divergence, so agreement of both the
current and the continuity equation with the standard Pauli limit is obtained.

\subsubsection{Spin Up and Spin Down}
The fixed right carrier already uses the unprimed third-axis projector
\(\pv{\Pi}(+)\). Where \(\vct{B}\neq\vct{0}\), its distinct direction is
therefore labeled with a prime. Its direction and projector are defined in
immediate succession by
\begin{align}
\vct{u}'&:=\frac{\vct{B}}{\|\vct{B}\|},
\qquad (\vct{u}')^2=1,
\label{eq:supporting-magnetic-axis}\\
\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)&:=\tfrac12(1\pm \vct{u}'\cdot\sigv).
\label{eq:supporting-magnetic-projectors}
\end{align}
The prime has the value-label meaning defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:prime-convention}.
The signed action obtained from these definitions is
\begin{equation*}
(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)=\pm\pv{\Pi}'(\pm).
\end{equation*}
The Pauli state is decomposed by
\begin{align*}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)
&=\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}},\\
(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)
&=\pm\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm),\\
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
&=\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(+)+\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(-),
\end{align*}
Thus \(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(+)\) and
\(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(-)\) are the spin-up and spin-down parts along
the field axis. The fixed-projector condition is preserved under both left
projectors:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)\pv{\Pi}(+)
&=\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)
\bigl(\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\pv{\Pi}(+)\bigr)\\
&=\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

At leading order, the Pauli equation is written as
\begin{align*}
\vct{P}&=-i\gradv-q\vct{A},\\
i\partial_t\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}
&=\left(\frac{\vct{P}^2}{2m}+qV\right)
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\notag\\
&\quad-\frac{q}{2m}\,\|\vct{B}\|(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}+O(m^{-2}).
\end{align*}
The following result is obtained by applying \(\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\) to the time
evolution:
\begin{align*}
i\partial_t\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)
&=\left(\frac{\vct{P}^2}{2m}+qV\right)
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)\notag\\
&\quad-\frac{q\|\vct{B}\|}{2m}
(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)\notag\\
&\quad+i\bigl(\partial_t\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\bigr)
\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}\notag\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2m}\com{\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)}{\vct{P}^2}
\notag\\
&\qquad\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_large}}+O(m^{-2}).
\end{align*}
Here \(qV\) and the local field-axis term commute at each position with
\(\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\); spatial variation is incorporated through the displayed
kinetic commutator. The two local spin parts are coupled by this term
together with \(\partial_t\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\).
If \(\vct{B}\) is uniform and static, then \(\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\) is constant and
the coupling term is zero, so the two spin parts evolve independently. In that case
\begin{equation*}
(-\vct{B}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)
=\mp \|\vct{B}\|\,\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm),
\end{equation*}
and the resulting magnetic energy separation is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&i\partial_t\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)\\
&\qquad=\left(
\frac{\vct{P}^2\mp q\|\vct{B}\|}{2m}+qV
\right)\pv{\psi}_{\eqtext{Pauli\_split}}(\pm)\\
&\qquad\quad+O(m^{-2}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
A preferred axis is therefore selected by the field direction, and opposite
energy shifts are acquired by the two projector parts. This magnetic
separation of spin energies is conventionally called the Zeeman effect
\cite{pauli1927,sakurai2017}.

\subsubsection{Free Energy and Momentum-Axis Checks}
The pair-valued function \(\mathcal H\) defined in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:dirac-hamiltonian} generates the free time evolution and is
conventionally called the Hamiltonian, or energy operator
\cite{greiner2000,sakurai2017}. Every division by \(E(\vct{p})\) in this
subsection assumes \(E(\vct{p})>0\); the massless zero-momentum case has no
energy-sign split of this form. For a pair-valued left action with
algebra-valued coefficients, the \(\Hop\) operation is defined intrinsically
by
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}})
&:=\bigl(
\pv{\mathcal O}(1,1)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}+\pv{\mathcal O}(1,2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2},\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad
\pv{\mathcal O}(2,1)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}+\pv{\mathcal O}(2,2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}
\bigr),\\
\mathcal O^{\Hop}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}})
&:=\bigl(
\herm{\pv{\mathcal O}(1,1)}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}
+\herm{\pv{\mathcal O}(2,1)}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2},\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad
\herm{\pv{\mathcal O}(1,2)}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}
+\herm{\pv{\mathcal O}(2,2)}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The exchanged input positions follow directly from reversal of product order
inside the two-block pairing. For the free Hamiltonian, this operation gives
\begin{align*}
\mathcal H^{\Hop}(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}})
&=\bigl(
-\herm{(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}+m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2},\notag\\
&\qquad
m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}+\herm{(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)}\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}
\bigr)\notag\\
&=\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}).
\end{align*}
Here
\(\herm{(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)}=\vct{p}\cdot\sigv\), and the real mass
terms are interchanged without change. For any two projected pairs
\(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left}}\) and
\(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right}}\), the pairing identity is
\begin{equation*}
\left\langle
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left}}
\middle|
\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right}})
\right\rangle
=
\left\langle
\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_left}})
\middle|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_right}}
\right\rangle.
\end{equation*}
The Hamiltonian is therefore Hermitian under the pair-valued spatial
pairing.

Direct composition of the ordered-pair action gives
\begin{align*}
\mathcal H(\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}))
&=\Bigl(
\bigl((\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)^2+m^2\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}\notag\\
&\qquad
+\bigl(-m(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)+m(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2},\notag\\
&\qquad
\bigl((\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)^2+m^2\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}\notag\\
&\qquad
+\bigl(-m(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)+m(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)\bigr)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1}
\Bigr)\notag\\
&=\bigl(
(m^2+\vct{p}^2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},1},\,
(m^2+\vct{p}^2)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac},2}
\bigr)\notag\\
&=E(\vct{p})^2\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}.
\end{align*}
The identity
\((\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)^2=\vct{p}^2\) follows directly from the sigma
product, so every mixed term cancels inside the ordered pair.

The two energy-sign parts are defined by
\begin{equation*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(\pm)
:=\frac12\left(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}
\pm\frac{\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}})}{E(\vct{p})}
\right).
\end{equation*}
The preceding square then gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal H\bigl(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(\pm)\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\frac12\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}})\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad\pm
\frac{\mathcal H(\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}))}
{2E(\vct{p})}\\
&\qquad=\frac12\mathcal H(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}})
\pm\frac12E(\vct{p})\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}\\
&\qquad=\pm E(\vct{p})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(\pm),\\
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(+)
+\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(-)
=\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Their orthogonality for \(E(\vct{p})>0\) follows from Hermiticity by
applying the Hamiltonian on either side of the pairing:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\left\langle
\mathcal H\bigl(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(+)\bigr)
\middle|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(-)
\right\rangle\\
&\qquad=
\left\langle
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(+)
\middle|
\mathcal H\bigl(\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(-)\bigr)
\right\rangle,\\
&E(\vct{p})
\left\langle
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(+)
\middle|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(-)
\right\rangle\\
&\qquad=-E(\vct{p})
\left\langle
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(+)
\middle|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(-)
\right\rangle,\\
&2E(\vct{p})
\left\langle
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(+)
\middle|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(-)
\right\rangle=0,\\
&\left\langle
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(+)
\middle|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy}}(-)
\right\rangle=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
A number that multiplies a nonzero state under an operator action is called
an eigenvalue. The two real energy eigenvalues are
\(\pm E(\vct{p})\) \cite{sakurai2017}.

Because the fixed right carrier remains \(\pv{\Pi}(+)\), the distinct
momentum-axis direction and projector pair are primed. For
\(\vct{p}\neq\vct{0}\), they are defined in the required order by
\begin{align}
\vct{u}'&:=\frac{\vct{p}}{\|\vct{p}\|},
\label{eq:supporting-momentum-axis}\\
\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)&:=\frac12(1\pm\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv),
\label{eq:supporting-momentum-projectors}\\
(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)
&=\pm\|\vct{p}\|\pv{\Pi}'(\pm).\notag
\end{align}
This is the construction in Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:momentum-axis} and
\eqref{eq:momentum-axis-projector}, with the prime carrying the value-label
meaning from Eq.\ \eqref{eq:prime-convention}.
The projector commutes with \(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv\), and its left action
commutes with the real mass factors in \(\mathcal H\). The positive- and
negative-energy axis parts are therefore defined separately:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)\\
&\qquad:=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy},1}(+),\\
&\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy},2}(+)
\end{aligned}
\right),\\
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)\\
&\qquad:=\left(
\begin{aligned}
&\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy},1}(-),\\
&\pv{\Pi}'(\pm)\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_energy},2}(-)
\end{aligned}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Their energy and momentum-axis equations are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal H\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=E(\vct{p})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm),\\
&\mathcal H\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=-E(\vct{p})
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis}}(\pm),\\
&(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm)
\\
&\qquad=\pm\|\vct{p}\|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm),\\
&(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm)
\\
&\qquad=\pm\|\vct{p}\|
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Writing the pair-valued energy equations one block at a time gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm)\\
&\qquad=\bigl(E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm),\\
&m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm)\\
&\qquad=\bigl(E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm),\\
&m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm)\\
&\qquad=\bigl(-E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm),\\
&m\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm)\\
&\qquad=\bigl(-E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:energy-axis-block-check}
\end{equation}
Multiplication of either matching pair of scalar factors recovers
\begin{align*}
m^2
&=\bigl(E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)
  \bigl(E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)\\
&=\bigl(-E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)
  \bigl(-E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)\\
&=E(\vct{p})^2-\vct{p}^2.
\end{align*}

For the positive-energy family, the two projected blocks have the intrinsic
amplitude expansions
\begin{align*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm)
&=a\pv{\Pi}(+)+b\pv{a}(-),\\
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm)
&=c\pv{\Pi}(+)+d\pv{a}(-).
\end{align*}
Their scalar and vector extractions are
\begin{align*}
\eqfunc{scalar}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm)
\bigr)&=\frac{a}{2},\\
\eqfunc{vector}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm)
\bigr)&=\frac12\vthree{b}{-ib}{a},\\
\eqfunc{scalar}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm)
\bigr)&=\frac{c}{2},\\
\eqfunc{vector}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm)
\bigr)&=\frac12\vthree{d}{-id}{c}.
\end{align*}
The negative-energy family has the same expansions with the distinct
coefficients \(a',b',c',d'\):
\begin{align*}
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm)
&=a'\pv{\Pi}(+)+b'\pv{a}(-),\\
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm)
&=c'\pv{\Pi}(+)+d'\pv{a}(-).
\end{align*}
The required left actions on the intrinsic carrier basis are
\begin{align*}
\pv{\sigma}_1\pv{\Pi}(+)&=\pv{a}(-),
&
\pv{\sigma}_1\pv{a}(-)&=\pv{\Pi}(+),\\
\pv{\sigma}_2\pv{\Pi}(+)&=i\pv{a}(-),
&
\pv{\sigma}_2\pv{a}(-)&=-i\pv{\Pi}(+),\\
\pv{\sigma}_3\pv{\Pi}(+)&=\pv{\Pi}(+),
&
\pv{\sigma}_3\pv{a}(-)&=-\pv{a}(-).
\end{align*}
Thus the momentum action on each block is obtained as
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},1}(\pm)
\\
&\qquad=\bigl(p_3a+(p_1-ip_2)b\bigr)
\pv{\Pi}(+)\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+\bigl((p_1+ip_2)a-p_3b\bigr)\pv{a}(-),\\
&(\vct{p}\cdot\sigv)
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},2}(\pm)
\\
&\qquad=\bigl(p_3c+(p_1-ip_2)d\bigr)
\pv{\Pi}(+)\\[-0.2em]
&\qquad\quad+\bigl((p_1+ip_2)c-p_3d\bigr)\pv{a}(-).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Equality of the two independent carrier amplitudes gives the four
positive-energy momentum-axis relations
\begin{align*}
p_3a+(p_1-ip_2)b&=\pm\|\vct{p}\|a,
&
(p_1+ip_2)a-p_3b&=\pm\|\vct{p}\|b,\\
p_3c+(p_1-ip_2)d&=\pm\|\vct{p}\|c,
&
(p_1+ip_2)c-p_3d&=\pm\|\vct{p}\|d.
\end{align*}
The negative-energy family satisfies the same four relations with
\(a,b,c,d\) replaced by \(a',b',c',d'\). The energy-axis equations in
Eq.\ \eqref{eq:energy-axis-block-check} give
\begin{align*}
mc&=\bigl(E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)a,
&
md&=\bigl(E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)b,\\
ma&=\bigl(E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)c,
&
mb&=\bigl(E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)d,\\
mc'&=\bigl(-E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)a',
&
md'&=\bigl(-E(\vct{p})\pm\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)b',\\
ma'&=\bigl(-E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)c',
&
mb'&=\bigl(-E(\vct{p})\mp\|\vct{p}\|\bigr)d'.
\end{align*}
These are the established free Dirac energy and momentum-axis spin
relations, obtained entirely from the projector--ladder basis.

The directional current follows directly from the intrinsic current
definition. The momentum-axis equation replaces each left action of
\(\vct{u}'\cdot\sigv\) by its sign, and the projector--ladder expansion gives
the two block densities. Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{j}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\pm\bigl(
|c|^2+|d|^2-|a|^2-|b|^2
\bigr),\\
&\rho\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2+|d|^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
For the negative-energy family, the same component calculation gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{j}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\pm\bigl(
|c'|^2+|d'|^2-|a'|^2-|b'|^2
\bigr),\\
&\rho\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)\\
&\qquad=|a'|^2+|b'|^2+|c'|^2+|d'|^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Use of the energy-axis amplitude relations then gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{
\vct{u}'\cdot\vct{j}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)}
{\rho\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)}
\\
&\qquad=\pm
\frac{|c|^2+|d|^2-|a|^2-|b|^2}
{|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2+|d|^2}\\
&\qquad=\frac{\|\vct{p}\|}{E(\vct{p})},\\
&\frac{
\vct{j}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)}
{\rho\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)}
\\
&\qquad=\frac{\vct{p}}{E(\vct{p})},\\
&\frac{
\vct{j}\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)}
{\rho\bigl(
\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis}}(\pm)
\bigr)}
\\
&\qquad=-\frac{\vct{p}}{E(\vct{p})}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus the current divided by the density is the expected relativistic
velocity for each energy-sign mode.

Every displayed operation is applied on the left. The fixed-projector
condition is therefore preserved directly:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm)
\pv{\Pi}(+)
\\
&\qquad=\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_positive\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm),\\
&\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm)
\pv{\Pi}(+)
\\
&\qquad=\bPsi_{\eqtext{Dirac\_negative\_energy\_axis},n}(\pm).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
No second projected carrier is introduced. The spin-axis sign measured along
the momentum direction is conventionally called helicity. At
\(\vct{p}=0\), no momentum direction exists and helicity is therefore
undefined; any separately chosen spin axis may still be used
\cite{greiner2000}.
