\subsubsection{Quaternion Products}
For \(a\in\R\) and \(\vct{B}\in\R^3\), a quaternion is the complex
paravector
\begin{equation}
\pv{Q}:=a+\vct{B}\cdot\vct{q}
=a-i\,\vct{B}\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:quaternion-paravector}
\end{equation}
The quaternion basis is fixed by star, so
\(\conj{\pv{Q}}=\pv{Q}\). The ordinary algebra square is therefore
\begin{align}
\pv{Q}\conj{\pv{Q}}
&=\pv{Q}^{2}\notag\\
&=(a+\vct{B}\cdot\vct{q})^{2}\notag\\
&=a^{2}-\vct{B}^{2}+2a\,\vct{B}\cdot\vct{q}\notag\\
&=a^{2}-\vct{B}^{2}-2ia\,\vct{B}\cdot\sigv.
\label{eq:quaternion-square}
\end{align}
Thus star is not the operation conventionally called quaternion
conjugation. In the present notation that operation is \(\Hop\):
\begin{equation*}
\herm{\pv{Q}}
=\adjf{\pv{Q}}
=a-\vct{B}\cdot\vct{q}
=a+i\,\vct{B}\cdot\sigv.
\end{equation*}
The determinant and the positive quaternion norm square consequently agree:
\begin{equation}
\detf{\pv{Q}}
=\pv{Q}\herm{\pv{Q}}
=\herm{\pv{Q}}\pv{Q}
=a^{2}+\vct{B}^{2}.
\label{eq:quaternion-norm}
\end{equation}
Here the norm square is the nonnegative real sum of the squared coefficients,
whereas Eq.\ \eqref{eq:quaternion-square} is generally another quaternion.
A quaternion satisfying \(\detf{\pv{Q}}=1\) is called a unit quaternion.

\subsubsection{Quaternion Form of the Rotor Law}
For the rotor \(\pv{R}(\theta,\vct{u})\) used in the preceding Rotation
subsection, the corresponding unit quaternion is
\begin{equation*}
\pv{Q}:=\herm{\pv{R}}
=\cos\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
+\sin\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\vct{u}\cdot\vct{q}.
\end{equation*}
Its Hermitian counterpart is \(\herm{\pv{Q}}=\pv{R}\). Multiplication of the
sigma-vector rotor law by the central scalar \(-i\) gives
\begin{align*}
\herm{\pv{R}}\,(\vct{r}\cdot\vct{q})\,\pv{R}
&=-i\,\herm{\pv{R}}\,(\vct{r}\cdot\sigv)\,\pv{R}\\
&=-i\,\vct{r}'\cdot\sigv
=\vct{r}'\cdot\vct{q}.
\end{align*}
The quaternion rotation law is therefore
\begin{equation}
\vct{r}'\cdot\vct{q}
=\pv{Q}\,(\vct{r}\cdot\vct{q})\,\herm{\pv{Q}}.
\label{eq:quaternion-rotation}
\end{equation}
This has the same positive rotation direction as the Rodrigues formula above.
If the alternative identification \(\pv{Q}=\pv{R}\) is made, the two
quaternion factors must instead be written in the opposite order. No second
rotation algebra is introduced by either convention.
